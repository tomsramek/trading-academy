The seed phrase is the one thing in this whole course you cannot afford to lose. Yet the backup has to
last for years – and a lot can happen in the meantime: fire, water, tidying up, moving house, a curious
visitor. A good backup survives all of it.

## What you protect against

| Threat                       | What helps                                         |
| ---------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| fire                         | metal (stainless steel), a second copy elsewhere   |
| water and damp               | metal, or paper in a waterproof cover              |
| fading and decay             | metal, or good paper and permanent ink             |
| a thief or a curious visitor | a discreet hiding place, no "bitcoin" label        |
| loss when tidying or moving  | a fixed place that you (and a trusted person) know |
| a digital leak               | never a photo, cloud, email or file                |

## Paper

The paper from the wallet's box is fine to start with: write legibly with permanent ink and put it in an
envelope and a waterproof cover. Its weaknesses are obvious – paper burns, gets wet and fades over the
years.

## Metal

Metal backups are plates or capsules in which you stamp the words or assemble them from letter tiles. The
first four letters of each word are enough; the word is uniquely identified by them.

- **Stainless steel** melts only at around 1,400 °C; a house fire does not usually get that hot.
- **Aluminium** melts at around 660 °C – it is not suitable as a backup against fire.

Metal costs tens of euros. For savings whose loss would hurt, it is cheap insurance.

## Where to store it and how many copies

- **One copy at home** in a fixed, discreet place – not in the drawer with the device.
- **A second copy elsewhere** – with a trusted person or in a bank safe deposit box. It protects you if
  you lose your home.
- **More copies = more places where someone can find it.** Two copies in two places are a sensible
  compromise.

Check the backup once a year: is it legible, complete and in its place?

<Callout type="risk" title="No digital backup">
  No photo on your phone, file on your computer, email, cloud or note in an app.
  Every device connected to the internet is a place the seed phrase can leak
  from – and you find out only when the money is gone.
</Callout>

## Common myths

- **"I have it in a safe, nothing can happen."** A safe protects against a thief, not always against fire
  and water.
- **"I will label it 'Bitcoin seed phrase' so my family finds it."** So will a thief. Instructions for your
  family belong elsewhere – that is lesson ten.
- **"One copy is enough."** One fire or one flood and you have nothing.

<Callout title="Summary">

- A backup must survive fire, water, time, thieves and tidying – and must never be digital.
- Paper is fine to start; for larger amounts stainless steel is better (not aluminium).
- Two copies in two places are a sensible compromise between loss and exposure.
- Once a year, check that the backup is legible, complete and in its place.

</Callout>
