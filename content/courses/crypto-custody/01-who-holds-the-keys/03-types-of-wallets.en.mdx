A wallet is like a pocket: you put change for coffee in your back pocket, not your salary. Crypto works
the same way – a wallet "in your pocket" for everyday payments and a safe for savings.

<Illustration name="walletPockets" />

## Hot wallets

A [hot wallet](/courses/crypto-basics/wallets) keeps its keys on a device connected to the internet:

- **Mobile app** – convenient, your phone is always with you. But phones get lost, stolen and filled with
  apps.
- **Browser extension** – the most convenient for blockchain apps. But the browser is where you meet the
  most scam websites.
- **Desktop program** – similar to a phone; a computer is as safe as the way you use it.

A hot wallet suits smaller amounts and everyday use.

## Cold wallets

A so-called <KeyTerm term="hardware-wallet">hardware wallet</KeyTerm> is a small device that never lets
the keys out. A computer or phone prepares the transaction, but the device signs it – and you confirm it
with a button or a touch on its screen. Even if the computer is infected, it cannot steal the key from the
wallet.

It costs roughly 50 to 250 euros depending on the model. Well-known brands include Trezor, Ledger, BitBox
or Keystone – mentioned only as examples, not as recommendations.

## How to choose

- **Screen** – it must be big enough to read the whole address and amount.
- **Supported networks** – does it support the cryptocurrencies you want to hold?
- **Open code** – with some manufacturers anyone can check the software, with others only part of it.
- **Secure chip** – it makes a physical attack harder if someone steals the device.
- **The manufacturer's reputation** – how it handled past problems and how quickly it releases fixes.

## Why not a paper wallet

It used to be recommended to print a private key on paper. Not any more:

- the printer and the computer the key was created on may be compromised,
- a paper wallet usually has a single address, and it is easy to make a mistake when withdrawing,
- you cannot sign anything safely with paper – you still have to type the key into some device.

Today paper makes sense as a **backup of the seed phrase**, not as a wallet. Backups are the topic of the
third module.

<Callout
  type="warning"
  title="A hardware wallet does not protect against everything"
>
  It protects the key from an infected computer. It does not protect you if you
  sign a fraudulent transaction, lose your backup or reveal your seed phrase to
  someone.
</Callout>

## Common myths

- **"A mobile wallet is as safe as a hardware one."** For small amounts it is enough, for savings it is not.
- **"The most expensive hardware wallet is the best."** The screen, network support and the manufacturer's
  reputation decide.
- **"A paper wallet is the safest because it is offline."** It is offline, but easy to make mistakes with.

<Callout title="Summary">

- Hot wallets (phone, browser, computer) are for smaller amounts and everyday use.
- A hardware wallet keeps the key inside and signs transactions itself – it is for savings.
- Choose by screen, supported networks, open code, chip and the manufacturer's reputation.
- Paper is for backing up a seed phrase today, not as a wallet.

</Callout>
