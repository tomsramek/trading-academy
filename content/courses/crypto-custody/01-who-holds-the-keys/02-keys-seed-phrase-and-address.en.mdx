People worry about dropping their hardware wallet in water. Yet the device is replaceable – you buy a new
one and restore it. Only one piece of paper is irreplaceable: the seed phrase. To see why, let us look at
what is actually in it.

<Illustration name="masterKey" />

## Key and address

- A so-called <KeyTerm term="private-key">private key</KeyTerm> is a huge secret number. Whoever knows it can sign
  a transaction, i.e. send coins.
- From the private key a **public key** is calculated mathematically, and from that an **address** – what
  you give people so they can send you money.
- The path goes one way only. A private key cannot be calculated from an address, so feel free to show
  your address to anyone.

## The seed phrase: one key to every door

If you had to back up every private key separately, you would go mad. That is why wallets use a <KeyTerm term="seed-phrase">seed phrase</KeyTerm>: 12 or 24 words from a list of 2,048 words under the <KeyTerm term="bip39">BIP39</KeyTerm> standard. All your keys are calculated
from it – for Bitcoin, Ethereum, Solana and other networks, and for any number
of accounts.

What follows from that:

- **Whoever has the seed phrase has everything.** They do not need your device, password or PIN.
- **A lost device does not matter, a lost phrase does.** With a backup of the phrase you restore
  everything on a new device.
- **The phrase works in other wallets too.** If the manufacturer goes bust, you restore it elsewhere.
  Some wallets, though, calculate keys by a slightly different path – if you do not see your balance
  after restoring, nothing may be lost.

## Why nobody can guess it

Twelve words out of 2,048 give so many combinations that all the computers in the world could not try
them all in billions of years. The weak spot is not the mathematics but the person: a photo on a phone, a
file in the cloud, paper in a drawer, a "technician" who asks for the phrase.

## What matters

- **Word order.** The same words in a different order are a completely different wallet.
- **Spelling.** The words come from a list, and each can be identified from its first four letters alone
  – which is why metal plates often have only those stamped.
- **The last word** contains a checksum. So do not invent your own phrase – let the device generate it.

<Callout type="risk" title="Never put a seed phrase into a computer or phone">
  No photos, notes, emails, cloud or password manager on a device connected to
  the internet. Anyone who wants you to type your seed phrase on a website or
  read it out over the phone is a scammer – always.
</Callout>

## Common myths

- **"The coins are in the device."** They are on the blockchain. The device only holds keys – and those
  can be restored from the phrase.
- **"I must keep my address secret."** An address is public. You must keep the seed phrase and private
  keys secret.
- **"A seed phrase is for one cryptocurrency only."** One phrase usually opens accounts on many networks.

<Callout title="Summary">

- A private key signs transactions; the public key and address are calculated from it, not the other way.
- A seed phrase (12 or 24 words, BIP39) creates all your keys on many networks.
- The device is replaceable, the seed phrase is not – it is what you back up.
- Word order and spelling matter; let the device generate the phrase.

</Callout>
