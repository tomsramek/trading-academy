Every indicator has knobs. The most important one is the period – how many candles the indicator takes
into account. Whoever turns the knobs by mood gets a different chart and a different "signal" every
time. So Professor Wick advises: turn them gently, and once you do, turn them once and for good.

## Where it is

On Binance's trading screen switch the chart to **Original** and click the indicator icon above the
chart. A dialog with two tabs opens:

- **Main Indicator** – drawn over the candles: MA, EMA, WMA, BOLL, VWAP, AVL, TRIX, SAR, SUPER.
- **Sub Indicator** – gets its own panel below the chart: VOL, MACD, RSI, MFI, KDJ, OBV, CCI, StochRSI, WR.

The checkbox switches an indicator on, the arrow on the right opens its settings. **Save** saves,
**Reset** restores the defaults.

## What the fields mean

- **Length (period)** – the number of candles. MA1 = 7 means the average of the last seven candles.
- **Close** – which price it is computed from. Almost always the close; you can also pick open, high or low.
- **Line style and colour** – looks only. Averages can have up to six lines at once (MA1 to MA6).
- **Levels** – reference lines for oscillators, such as 70 and 30 for RSI.

On a daily chart the period is in days, on an hourly chart in hours. MA 7 on an hourly chart is the
average of the last seven **hours**, not days.

## Two modes, two sets of defaults

Binance has two chart modes, and each has different defaults:

| Indicator | Original (Binance's own chart) | Trading View  |
| --------- | ------------------------------ | ------------- |
| RSI       | three lines: 6, 12 and 24      | one line: 14  |
| CCI       | 9                              | 20            |
| MFI       | three lines: 7, 14 and 21      | 14            |
| MA        | 7, 25 and 99                   | as you choose |

The charts in this course use the common values, like the Trading View mode (RSI 14, CCI 20, MFI 14).
When you see different numbers on Binance, first check which period the indicator has – the number in
brackets, such as RSI(6), tells you.

  If you tune the period until the indicator would have looked great in the

  your trading plan and do not change them after every loss.

## Common myths

- **"RSI on Binance shows 85 but the lesson says 72 – someone is wrong."** You probably have a different period: RSI(6) swings more than RSI(14).
- **"There is a best setting."** There are common settings. None works all the time.
- **"The settings are saved to my account."** Logged out, they are remembered only in the browser; clearing the browser data wipes them.
