The chart menu has more than twenty indicators, and each looks like a secret weapon. Good news: none of
them is magic. Each is an ordinary recipe – it takes a few recent candles, computes something from their
prices and draws the result as a line. Professor Wick calls it a cookbook: candles in, line out.

<Illustration name="candleRecipe" />

## A recipe for a line

The simplest indicator is the <KeyTerm term="moving-average">moving average</KeyTerm>: add up the
closing prices of the last N candles and divide by N. The result is one point. Then the window moves by
one candle and you compute again. Point by point, a line appears. Click **Next** and watch it grow:

Notice two things. The line is made only from **past** candles – there is nothing about tomorrow in it.
And it is **smoother** than the price: one wild day melts away in an average of five.

## Every indicator lags

Because the average also counts older candles, it turns only after the price has turned. This is called
lag. The more candles an indicator takes, the smoother it is – and the more it lags:

That is not a bug, it is the price of smoothness. A fast indicator gives a signal early, but often a
false one. A slow one is wrong less often, but comes late. This whole course keeps coming back to it.

## Repainting: the last candle lies

The last candle on the chart is not finished yet – the price is still moving in it. And with it moves the
last value of every indicator. A signal you see in the middle of the day can be gone by midnight. This
is called <KeyTerm term="repainting">repainting</KeyTerm>.

The standard indicators in an exchange's menu no longer change a **closed** candle. So the rule is
simple: a signal counts once the candle has closed.

<Callout type="warning" title="An indicator does not see the future">
  An indicator is another view of the same past prices. It adds no new
  information and does not know what the price will do tomorrow. It can help you
  organise the chart and keep to your rules – not predict.
</Callout>

## Common myths

- **"The indicator knows something the price does not."** It is computed only from the price (and maybe volume). Different glasses, not a different world.
- **"A faster indicator is better."** It is faster – and wrong more often. A slower one is calmer – and later.
- **"A signal on a running candle counts."** It counts once the candle closes. Until then it can change.

<Callout title="Summary">

- An indicator is a formula: it computes a point from candles, moves one candle on and computes again.
- It is computed only from the past, so it always lags behind the price.
- A longer period = a smoother line and more lag.
- The last, unfinished candle repaints; a signal counts once it closes.

</Callout>
