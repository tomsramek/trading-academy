highest. The <KeyTerm term="stochastic">stochastic oscillator</KeyTerm> asks only one thing: on which
floor did the price close today? On the roof it is 100, on the ground floor 0. Professor Wick likes
riding the lift – he just does not know where it goes tomorrow.

## How %K is computed

1. Find the highest high and the lowest low of the last 14 candles.
2. %K = (close − low) / (high − low) × 100.
3. The %D line is the average of the last 3 %K values – calmer and a little behind.

Above 80 the close is near the top of the range, below 20 near the bottom. Walk through the calculation
– the green and red dashed lines show the window's high and low:

## KDJ: the stochastic with a third line

In Binance's Original-mode menu you will not find a plain stochastic – there is its relative <KeyTerm term="kdj">KDJ</KeyTerm> with the setting **9, 3, 3**. It computes the same "position in the
range" from 9 candles, but smooths it by thirds:

- **K** = two thirds of the previous K + a third of the new position in the range (it starts at 50),
- **D** = the same smoothing, this time of K,
- **J** = 3 × K − 2 × D – it runs ahead of the others and can shoot above 100 or below 0.

On the 2023–2024 Bitcoin chart the J line was above 100 on 39 days and below zero on 11 days. That is not
a calculation error, that is J.

On Binance you switch it on in **Original** mode: the indicator icon, the **Sub Indicator** tab, tick
**KDJ**, **Save**.

## Oversold can get more oversold

On 2 August 2024 %K fell from 39 to 3 – the close of 61,498 was almost at the bottom of the two-week
range. "Oversold, buy"? Three days later the price was down at 49,000. The bottom of the range simply
moved lower.

<Callout type="warning" title="A position in the range is not a floor">
  The stochastic shows the position against past candles. When the market breaks
  down, the new low creates a new range, and an "oversold" oscillator can stay
  down for days. Buying just because it is below 20 is catching a falling knife.
</Callout>

## Common myths

- **"%K below 20 = the bottom."** It is the bottom of the last 14 candles, not of the market.
- **"KDJ and the stochastic are two independent opinions."** They are two versions of the same calculation.
- **"J above 100 is an error."** J = 3K − 2D, so it has no 0–100 limits. It is the amplified, fastest line.

<Callout title="Summary">

- %K = (close − low) / (high − low) × 100 over the last N candles; %D is its average.
- Binance offers KDJ 9, 3, 3: K and D smoothed by thirds, J = 3K − 2D.
- Above 80 the close is near the top of the range, below 20 near the bottom.
- An oversold market can keep falling – the range moves down with it.

</Callout>
