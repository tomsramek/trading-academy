Stretch a rubber band from the average price. The further the price moves from the average, the more the
band stretches – and that is exactly what <KeyTerm term="cci">CCI</KeyTerm> measures. Unlike RSI it has
no ceiling: the band stretches to +300 just as well as to +100. Professor Wick advises not to stand
right in front of it.

<Illustration name="rubberBand" />

## How it is computed

1. The **typical price** of each candle = (high + low + close) / 3.
2. Compute its average over the last N candles.
3. Compute the **mean deviation**: how much the typical price differs from that average on average
   (ignoring the sign).
4. CCI = (typical price − average) / (0.015 × mean deviation).

Its author Donald Lambert chose the 0.015 constant so that roughly 70–80% of values fall between −100
and +100. That holds for "well-behaved" markets. Bitcoin is not well behaved: in 2023–2024 only about
56% of CCI 20 values fell within ±100.

## Where to find it on Binance

In **Original** mode: the indicator icon, the **Sub Indicator** tab, tick **CCI**, **Save**. The legend
shows **CCI(9)** – in this mode Binance's default period is 9. In Trading View mode and in most
textbooks CCI 20 is used; the course charts draw the same.

## How to read CCI

- **Above +100**: the price has moved unusually far above the average. In a strong rise CCI can stay there a long time.
- **Below −100**: the same downwards.
- **Within ±100**: the price stays in its usual band around the average.

Lambert invented CCI for commodities with cycles – hence "Commodity" in the name. On crypto it works like
any oscillator: it describes how stretched a move is, not where it goes.

<Callout type="warning" title="No ceiling, no certainty">
  CCI +200 does not mean "now it has to fall". Strong trends stretch the band
  further than you would expect. Trading just because CCI is "too high" is a bet
  against the trend.
</Callout>

## Common myths

- **"CCI runs from 0 to 100 like RSI."** It has no limits; ±100 are only guide lines.
- **"CCI 9 and CCI 20 show the same thing."** The shorter period swings more and breaks ±100 more often.
- **"It is an indicator for commodities, it does not work on crypto."** It works just as well – and just as limitedly – as other oscillators.

<Callout title="Summary">

- CCI = (typical price − its average) / (0.015 × mean deviation).
- ±100 are guide lines; beyond them the price is unusually far from its average.
- Binance (Original) defaults to CCI 9; CCI 20 is common elsewhere.
- CCI has no upper or lower limit.

</Callout>
