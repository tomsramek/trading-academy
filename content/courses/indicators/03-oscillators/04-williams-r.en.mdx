Larry Williams took the stochastic, turned it upside down and gave it negative numbers. The result is
called <KeyTerm term="williams-r">Williams %R</KeyTerm>, and on Binance you find it as **WR**. Professor
Wick studies it hanging by his feet – he says it is clearer that way.

<Illustration name="upsideDown" />

## How it is computed

1. Find the highest high and the lowest low of the last 14 candles.
2. %R = (high − close) / (high − low) × (−100).

When the price closes at the high of the range, %R is **0**. At the low it is **−100**. The levels are
therefore written as negatives: above **−20** the close is near the top of the range (like the
stochastic above 80), below **−80** near the bottom (like below 20).

And here is the punchline: %R + 100 = the unsmoothed stochastic %K. It is exactly the same number,
shifted by a hundred. On the last day of our data %R was −86.4 and the stochastic 13.6.

## Where to find it on Binance

In **Original** mode: the indicator icon, the **Sub Indicator** tab, tick **WR**, **Save**. The legend
in the panel shows **Wm %R(14)** – a period of 14 candles.

<Callout type="warning" title="Two oscillators, one piece of information">
  Williams %R and the stochastic are not two independent confirmations. When
  both agree on "oversold", you still know only one thing: the price is near the
  bottom of the recent range. A second opinion only comes from another family.
</Callout>

## Common myths

- **"−90 is terribly low, the market is dead."** It only means the close is near the low of the last 14 candles.
- **"%R is better than the stochastic."** It is the same calculation with a different scale.
- **"A negative number = the price is falling."** The whole scale is negative; −20 is near the top.

<Callout title="Summary">

- %R = (high − close) / (high − low) × (−100) over the last N candles.
- 0 is at the high of the range, −100 at the low; the levels are −20 and −80.
- %R + 100 = the unsmoothed stochastic %K.
- On Binance it is WR, with the legend Wm %R(14).

</Callout>
