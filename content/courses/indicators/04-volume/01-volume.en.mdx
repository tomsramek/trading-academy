The price tells you where the market moved. Volume tells you how many people were there. It is the
difference between the news "something is happening in the square" and a photo that shows whether three
people stand there or thirty thousand. Professor Wick has a simple rule for volume: first look at who is
behind the move.

## What one bar is

Trading <KeyTerm term="volume">volume</KeyTerm> is the amount traded in a given candle.
On Binance, for the BTC/USDT pair it is counted in bitcoin: a bar of 40,000
under a daily candle means 40,000 BTC changed hands on this exchange that day.
Every trade has a buyer and a seller, so volume does not say whether more people
bought or sold – only how much was traded.

The colour of the bar just copies the candle: green under a candle that closed higher than it opened,
red under one that closed lower.

## VOL on Binance

In Original mode, open the indicator icon above the chart and the **Sub Indicator** tab. **VOL** is
usually on as soon as you open the chart. Its settings have two lines: **MAVOL1 = 7** and
**MAVOL2 = 14**. Binance describes them as adding up the volume over a period and dividing by the number
of candles – an ordinary <KeyTerm term="moving-average">moving average</KeyTerm>, just of volume instead
of price.

The volume averages answer whether today's bar is **unusually** high. A bar above both lines means
more was traded today than in recent days.

Three examples from the chart:

- **On 5 March 2024** the price opened at 68,246, fell as low as 59,005 and closed at 63,724. 132,697
  BTC were traded – about **2.9 times** the average of the previous 20 days (46,120 BTC). Both the fall
  and the bounce had a crowd behind them.
- **On 5 August 2024** volume was the highest of the whole period: 162,066 BTC, almost **6 times** the
  average of the previous 20 days (28,032 BTC). The price fell from 58,161 to 54,019 that day.
- **On 6 November 2024** the price jumped from 69,372 to 75,572 and broke above the previous highs.
  Volume of 104,127 BTC was about **4 times** the average. A breakout with a crowd behind it.

In general: a breakout through an important level on high volume is more convincing than one hardly
anyone noticed. And a move on low volume reverses more easily. More in the lesson
[Volume: confirming the move](/courses/trading-with-a-plan/volume).

## One exchange's volume is not the whole market

Binance shows only trades on Binance. Bitcoin trades on dozens of exchanges and volume differs between
them – even depending on where a zero-fee promotion is running. So compare volume **over time on the
same exchange**, not numbers from different exchanges with each other.

<Callout type="warning" title="Volume does not tell the direction">
  High volume means a lot was traded – not that a lot was bought. Every purchase
  has a sale on the other side. A big green bar after a long rise can just as
  well be enthusiasm as people taking profits.
</Callout>

## Common myths

- **"A green bar = more people bought."** The colour only copies the candle. There are always as many buyers as sellers.
- **"High volume = the price will keep going the same way."** The volume peak often comes at the end of a move – in panic or euphoria.
- **"Volume on one exchange is Bitcoin's volume."** It is that one exchange's volume. Compare it only with itself.
