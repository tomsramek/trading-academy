The price climbs to a new high, everyone celebrates – and the oscillator below the chart looks as if it
does not care. Professor Wick knows the feeling: when a runner gets further than last time but gasps for
air even more, the next lap may not happen. This mismatch is called <KeyTerm term="divergence">divergence</KeyTerm>.

<Illustration name="divergenceArrows" />

## Bearish and bullish divergence

- **Bearish divergence:** the price makes a **higher high**, but the oscillator (say <KeyTerm term="rsi">RSI</KeyTerm>) only a **lower peak**. The rise goes on, but with less strength.
- **Bullish divergence:** the price makes a **lower low**, but the oscillator a **higher trough**. The fall goes on, but the sellers are running out of breath.

There is also so-called hidden divergence: the price makes a higher low, but the oscillator a lower one.
It is read as a hint that the trend continues. The ordinary kind is enough to start with.

## A real example: December 2024

On 22 November 2024 Bitcoin climbed to **99,588** with RSI 14 at **82.2**. Less than a month later, on 17
December, it made a new high of **108,353** – but RSI reached only **70.1**. Price higher, strength lower:

## Why divergence often fails

In a strong trend a divergence can last for weeks. The price makes a higher high, then another and
another – and RSI below is a little lower each time. Whoever sold on the first divergence missed the rest
of the rise. Divergence does not say **when** the trend turns, or **whether** it will at all.

It is wiser to treat it as a heads-up: pay attention, tighten your risk, do not open a new trade in the
direction of a weakening move. If you want to trade against the trend, wait for confirmation – for
example a close below an important level or below a trend line.

<Callout type="warning" title="Divergence is not an order">
  Divergence looks beautiful in hindsight. In real time you do not know whether
  the last high has already come. Trading on divergence alone against a strong
  trend is one of the most expensive habits of new traders.
</Callout>

## Common myths

- **"Divergence = reversal."** It often means only a slowdown or a while sideways.
- **"The more divergences in a row, the surer the fall."** A string of divergences in a strong trend is common, and the trend can keep going.
- **"I can find divergence on every chart."** If you look for it, you will find it almost everywhere. So define in advance exactly what counts as one.

<Callout title="Summary">

- Bearish divergence: a higher high in price, a lower peak in the oscillator. Bullish: a lower low in price, a higher trough in the oscillator.
- December 2024: a high of 108,353 with RSI 70.1 against 99,588 with RSI 82.2 – then a fall towards 92,000.
- Divergence says a move is weakening, not when or whether it turns.
- Treat it as a warning and wait for confirmation, not as a signal to trade.
