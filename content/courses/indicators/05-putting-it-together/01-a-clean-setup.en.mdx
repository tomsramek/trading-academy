We have been through the whole indicator menu, and now comes the hardest discipline: switching most of
them off again. Professor Wick has brought a broom. He keeps on the chart only what answers a different
question from the rest – and sweeps everything else off the desk.

## Where we start

When you open a chart, Binance shows three averages, **MA 7, 25 and 99**, and **volume** below the
chart. Most people then add an oscillator – say RSI, and MACD on top:

It looks professional. But RSI and <KeyTerm term="macd">MACD</KeyTerm> mostly measure one thing: how fast
the price has been moving lately. When RSI shoots up, MACD usually does too. Two panels, largely one piece
of information.

## One from each family

A clearer chart comes from picking one member of each family:

1. **Trend** – one line over the candles, say SMA 50 or EMA 25. It answers: which way is it going?
2. **Oscillator** – one, say <KeyTerm term="rsi">RSI</KeyTerm> 14. It answers: is the move overdone?
3. **Volume** – the bars below the chart. It answers: is there interest behind the move?

It does not have to be this exact trio. What matters is that the indicators **do not overlap** – and that
you know which question each one answers.

## A checklist

- **Timeframe.** Settings for a daily chart do not automatically fit an hourly one. Pick a timeframe and stick to it.
- **One question, one indicator.** If you cannot say in one sentence why you have an indicator, switch it off.
- **Fixed settings.** Write the periods into your <KeyTerm term="trading-plan">trading plan</KeyTerm> and do not change them to suit the moment.
- **Rules in advance.** Write down what you will do when an indicator gives a signal – and what when two disagree.
- **Less is more.** Three lines read in five seconds. Eight lines take half an hour, and then a feeling decides anyway.

How to make indicators part of a whole plan is shown in the course
[Trading with a plan](/courses/trading-with-a-plan) – and why it pays not to trust them blindly, in the
lesson [Why indicators mislead](/courses/trading-with-a-plan/why-indicators-mislead).

<Callout type="warning" title="Tuning the settings is a trap">
  If you keep changing the periods until an indicator fits a past chart
  "perfectly", you have tuned it to chance. On future prices it usually fails.
  Choose sensible settings once and judge them over many trades, not one chart.
</Callout>

## Common myths

- **"RSI and MACD confirm each other."** They largely measure the same thing, so they almost always agree.
- **"Professionals have ten indicators on their chart."** Many have one or two – and clear rules.
- **"I will find settings that always work."** There are none. Every setting is a compromise between speed and calm.

<Callout title="Summary">

- One from each family: a trend line, an oscillator, volume.
- RSI and MACD partly overlap – they are not two independent confirmations.
- For every indicator, know the question it answers.
- Settings and rules belong in your trading plan, in advance and fixed.

</Callout>
