Bollinger Bands are a rubber tube around the price. When the market is calm, the tube deflates and hugs
the candles. When it gets wild, it inflates. Professor Wick once tried to stuff the whole chart into it –
almost nine candles out of ten fit, no more.

<Illustration name="bollingerTube" />

## How they are computed

The <KeyTerm term="bollinger-bands">Bollinger Bands</KeyTerm> have three lines:

- **middle** (MB) – the simple average of 20 closes,
- **upper** (UP) – middle + 2 × standard deviation,
- **lower** (DN) – middle − 2 × standard deviation.

The standard deviation measures how far from the average the prices in the window typically are. When
the price moves wildly it is large and the bands spread apart. When the price stands still it is small
and the bands come together. Go through the calculation:

Notice two things. In a strong trend the price "walks the band" – it closes at the upper edge or above
it day after day. And the bands are no wall: on the daily Bitcoin chart the price ended outside them
roughly one day in seven.

## The squeeze: quiet before a move

When the bands are very narrow, the market is unusually calm. Such a narrowing is called a squeeze, and
a big move often follows. Find it:

## What the settings do

The length changes how many candles the average and the deviation are computed from; the multiplier
changes how far away the bands are. With a multiplier of 2 more prices stay inside than with 1, but
never all of them. Move the length:

  data={btc}
  indicator="bollinger"

<Callout type="warning" title="Touching a band is not a signal">
  The price at the upper band does not mean "expensive, sell", and at the lower
  band "cheap, buy". In a strong trend the price sticks to a band for weeks. The
  bands show how wild the market is, not where it will go.
</Callout>

## Common myths

- **"95% of prices lie inside the bands."** That holds for a normal distribution, which prices do not have. On Bitcoin almost one day in seven ended outside.
- **"A squeeze shows the direction."** It only shows that something is coming. You have to look for the direction elsewhere.
- **"Touching the lower band is a cheap buy."** In a sell-off the price happily slides further along the lower band.
