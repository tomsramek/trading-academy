brought more money decides more. Professor Wick protested for a while – then he found out that a whale
with two bitcoins simply outvotes ten small traders with fractions.

## Three trades, two averages

The <KeyTerm term="vwap">VWAP</KeyTerm> (volume-weighted average price) multiplies
each price by how much was traded at it. Picture three trades:

| Trade | Price  | Amount  | Price × amount |
| ----- | ------ | ------- | -------------- |
| 1     | 60,000 | 0.1 BTC | 6,000          |
| 2     | 61,000 | 0.1 BTC | 6,100          |
| 3     | 59,000 | 2 BTC   | 118,000        |

- **Ordinary average:** (60,000 + 61,000 + 59,000) / 3 = **60,000**.
- **VWAP:** (6,000 + 6,100 + 118,000) / 2.2 BTC = **59,136**.

The big trade at 59,000 pulled VWAP almost all the way to itself. VWAP therefore answers: **how much,
on average, did the people who actually traded pay?** On a chart, instead of individual trades it uses
each candle's typical price (high + low + close) / 3 and its <KeyTerm term="volume">volume</KeyTerm>.

## A window or a day?

This is where platforms differ:

- **Binance** computes VWAP from the last **14 candles** (the Length 14 setting) – the window moves like
  a moving average's.
- **Many other platforms** compute VWAP from the start of the day (session VWAP) and start again at
  midnight.

Both versions are honest; they just answer a different question: "what was paid over the last 14
candles" versus "what was paid today". On a daily chart mainly the first makes sense – and even so,
VWAP is at home on hourly and shorter charts.

When the price is above VWAP, those who bought over the last 14 hours are on average in profit; below
it, at a loss. That is why mainly short-term traders watch it as the "fair price" of the last few hours.

<Callout type="warning" title="VWAP is not price support">
  VWAP shows the average price at which people traded recently – nothing more.
  The price can cross it back and forth several times a day. And since Binance
  and others compute it differently, the same chart can show two different
  "VWAP" lines.
</Callout>

## Common myths

- **"VWAP is just another moving average."** An average counts every candle the same; VWAP counts by volume.
- **"VWAP is the same everywhere."** Binance uses a window of 14 candles, other platforms start from zero every day.
- **"VWAP suits a weekly chart."** On long timeframes it loses its meaning – it is a tool for hours, not months.

<Callout title="Summary">

- VWAP = sum of (typical price × volume) / sum of volume; big trades weigh more.
- Binance computes VWAP from the last 14 candles, other platforms often from the start of the day.
- It suits hourly and shorter charts best.
- On Binance: Original → indicator icon → Main Indicator → VWAP (Length 14).

</Callout>
