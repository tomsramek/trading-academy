Parabolic SAR looks like a trail of tiny footsteps under the candles. Every day it gets a bit closer to
the price, and the longer the trend lasts, the bigger its steps. As soon as the price catches it, the
dots jump to the other side – like Professor Wick stepping on a rake.

## How it is computed

SAR stands for stop and reverse. <KeyTerm term="parabolic-sar">Parabolic SAR</KeyTerm> draws dots below
the price in a rise and above it in a fall. Every candle the dot moves closer to the trend's extreme (the
highest high in a rise, the lowest low in a fall):

**new dot = dot + AF × (extreme − dot)**

AF is the acceleration factor. It starts at **0.02** and grows by **0.02** with every new extreme of the
trend – up to a maximum of **0.2**. At the start of a trend the dot covers 2% of the remaining distance,
after ten new highs 20%. That is why the dots approach the price along a curve – hence "parabolic".

## The flip

When the price crosses a dot, the trend is over according to SAR: the dots jump to the other side, start
from the last extreme and AF goes back to 0.02. That is how SAR works as a moving stop – much like a <KeyTerm term="trailing-stop">trailing stop</KeyTerm> that follows the price.

## Strong in a trend, weak in a chop

In a clear trend SAR holds a trade for a long time and tightens the stop faster than a person would by
hand. In a market without a trend, though, the price crosses the dots back and forth, and every flip is
a new signal – mostly a false one. Between June and mid-August 2024 SAR flipped nine times while the
price swung between 49,000 and 72,000 without a clear direction.

## On Binance

**Original** mode → indicator icon → **Main Indicator** → **SAR**. You set the starting factor (Start,
default 0.02), the maximum (Maximum, default 0.2) and the colour of the dots:

Higher factors pull the dots in faster – more flips, more false signals. Lower ones slow them down.

<Callout type="warning" title="SAR is always in the market">
  SAR never says "stay out". It always shows either a rise or a fall – even in a
  market that has no trend at all. Anyone trading every flip would pay fees and
  losses for each of them in a sideways market.
</Callout>

## Common myths

- **"A SAR flip is a signal to trade."** In a sideways market it is mostly noise.
- **"The dots are a support level."** They are points computed by a formula, not a place where buyers stand.
- **"A higher maximum AF = a better SAR."** A faster SAR just flips more often.

<Callout title="Summary">

- New dot = dot + AF × (extreme − dot); AF 0.02, +0.02 with every new extreme, max. 0.2.
- When the price crosses a dot, the dots jump to the other side and AF starts again.
- In a trend it works like a moving stop; in a sideways market it swings.
- On Binance: Original → indicator icon → Main Indicator → SAR (0.02 / 0.2).
