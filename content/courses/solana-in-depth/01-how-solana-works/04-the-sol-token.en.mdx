SOL is the fuel of the network: it pays fees, it is locked up in staking and it gives validators their
voting weight. The smallest unit is called a lamport – one SOL has a billion lamports. Unlike Bitcoin,
though, SOL has no fixed cap on the number of coins.

<Illustration name="bathtubSupply" />

## Inflation: a tap that slowly closes

Every epoch new SOL is created and goes to validators and to those who stake with them. That is called <KeyTerm term="token-inflation">token inflation</KeyTerm>. It started in 2021 at
8% a year and under the original plan fell by 15% of its value every year, down
to a long-term floor of 1.5%.

In August 2026 validators narrowly voted to make it fall twice as fast – by 30% a year. Inflation is now
expected to reach the 1.5% floor around 2029. At the time of writing (October 2026) it is just under 4% a year.

## Burning: the drain in the bathtub

Half of every base fee is **burned** – gone forever. The other half goes to the validator, as do all
priority fees. Burned SOL lowers the number of coins, but in normal operation it is far less than what
inflation adds. The bathtub fills faster than it drains.

## Staking: why unstaked SOL loses its share

About two thirds of all SOL is staked, and at the time of writing the yield is around 5–6% a year. It
sounds like interest, but much of that "yield" is just a share of newly printed SOL:

- **Whoever stakes** has more SOL at the end of the year and their share of all SOL rises slightly.
- **Whoever does not stake** has the same number of SOL, but their share falls by the inflation rate
  every year.

The real gain above inflation is only about 1–2% a year – and that is in SOL, not in your own currency.
If the price of SOL halves, no staking makes up for it. Module 3 covers staking in detail.

## Who owns SOL

At the network's launch a large share of SOL went to the founders, early investors and the foundation.
One of the biggest holders was the FTX exchange together with the Alameda fund. When FTX went bankrupt in
November 2022, the market feared its SOL would all be sold at once:

From a high of 38.79 USD on 5 November, SOL fell to 12.37 USD in four days and to 8 USD by the end of the
year. In 2024 the FTX bankruptcy estate then sold a large part of its SOL at a discount to investment
funds – on terms that release the tokens gradually so they do not flood the market.

## Common myths

- **"Staking is risk-free 6% interest."** Most of it is a share of inflation, and the result in your own
  currency depends on the price.
- **"Fee burning makes SOL deflationary."** In normal operation inflation is far bigger than burning.
- **"FTX is history, it no longer affects SOL."** Gradually released tokens are supply the market has to
  absorb.

<Callout title="Summary">

- SOL pays fees and secures the network through staking; it has no fixed cap.
- Inflation started at 8% and falls towards 1.5%; a vote in August 2026 doubled the pace; just under 4% at the time of writing.
- Half of base fees are burned, but inflation is far bigger than burning.
- Staking mainly protects your share from inflation; it does not offset price risk.

</Callout>
