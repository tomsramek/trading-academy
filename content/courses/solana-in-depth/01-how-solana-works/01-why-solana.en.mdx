In 2017 Ethereum was choking. One popular game about collecting virtual cats flooded the network,
transactions waited and fees rose sharply. Around the same time, a former Qualcomm engineer, Anatoly Yakovenko, wrote a design
for a blockchain that would handle thousands of transactions per second for a fraction of a cent. That is
how Solana was born – its main network launched in March 2020.

## What is different

Like [Ethereum](/courses/crypto-basics/ethereum-and-smart-contracts), Solana is a blockchain for <KeyTerm term="smart-contract">smart contracts</KeyTerm> – programs that run on
the network. But it is built differently:

- **One big blockchain.** Ethereum moves most of its traffic to so-called second layers. Solana wants to
  handle everything in one network, so apps do not have to bridge money between layers.
- **Short blocks.** A new block was produced roughly every 0.4 seconds from launch, and from October 2026 it is meant to be every 0.2 seconds. On Ethereum it is 12 seconds, on
  Bitcoin 10 minutes on average.
- **Low fees.** The base fee is 0.000005 SOL per signature – a fraction of a cent even at a SOL price of
  200 USD.
- **Parallel processing.** Transactions that do not touch the same data run at the same time, not one
  after another.

## The blockchain trilemma

Blockchains are often described by a trilemma: you want **speed**, **security** and **decentralisation**,
but you only get two of them perfectly. Bitcoin bets on security and decentralisation – a block every ten
minutes, a node runs on an ordinary computer. Solana bets on speed.

The price of speed is **demanding hardware**. A so-called <KeyTerm term="validator">validator</KeyTerm> –
a computer that verifies and writes blocks on Solana – needs a powerful server, lots of memory and a fast
connection. Few people run such a machine at home, which is why there are far fewer validators than on
Bitcoin or Ethereum. More on that in the lesson on outages and decentralisation.

<Callout type="warning" title="Speed is not the same as value">
  A faster and cheaper network is a technical property, not a reason for the
  token to rise. The price of SOL depends on demand, market mood and supply –
  and it has already fallen by 97% once.
</Callout>

## Common myths

- **"Solana is the Ethereum killer."** Both networks solve the same problem differently and both have
  weaknesses. No winner is certain.
- **"Cheap fees = cheap use without risk."** Cheap transactions attract scammers and spam too.
- **"A fast network never goes down."** Solana has had several outages – the third lesson is about them.

<Callout title="Summary">

- Solana was created as an answer to slow and expensive blockchains around 2017; its main network has run
  since 2020.
- It bets on one fast blockchain with blocks every fraction of a second (0.4 seconds, from October 2026 0.2 seconds) and low fees.
- By the blockchain trilemma, it pays for speed with demanding hardware and fewer validators.
- Speed is a technical property, not a guarantee of a rising price.

</Callout>
