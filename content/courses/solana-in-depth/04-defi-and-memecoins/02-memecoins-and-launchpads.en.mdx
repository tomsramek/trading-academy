Solana became the home of memecoins – tokens with no use at all, whose price rests only on attention. The
reason is simple: creating a token is cheap and trading is fast. Since 2024 so-called launchpads have made
it even easier: you create a token in a few clicks. The result: tens of thousands of new "opportunities"
every day.

## How a launchpad works

The best-known launchpad, pump.fun (mentioned as an example), launched a simple model in January 2024:

1. Anyone creates a token for a fraction of a SOL – a name, a picture, done.
2. At first the token trades on a so-called <KeyTerm term="bonding-curve">bonding curve</KeyTerm>: the more
   people buy, the higher the price, according to a preset formula.
3. When the market value reaches a set threshold, the token "graduates" – it moves into a liquidity pool
   on a decentralised exchange and keeps trading there.

## How many tokens survive

According to public analyses of blockchain data, only about **1%** of tokens – often fewer – "graduate". In April 2026
more than 760,000 were created on a single launchpad, and roughly one in 120 made it further. And even
graduating is no win – most of the successful tokens then lose most of their value within days and weeks.

## Who makes money

- **Creators and insiders** – buy at the start at the lowest price, often through several wallets, and
  sell to those who come after them.
- **Bots** – buy in the first second after creation and sell at the first rise.
- **Platforms** – take a fee on every trade, whether the price rises or falls.
- **Buyers who arrive late** are the ones who pay for all of it.

Famous names are no protection either. The TRUMP token, launched in January 2025, helped push SOL to a
record 295.83 USD on 19 January – and itself lost more than 80% of its value within a few months. The
LIBRA token, promoted by Argentina's president in February 2025, fell more than 80% within hours.

## What a scam looks like

A so-called <KeyTerm term="rug-pull">rug pull</KeyTerm> means the creator "pulls the rug" and leaves
buyers with worthless tokens. The most common forms:

- the creator sells all their tokens at once,
- removes the liquidity from the pool, so there is nobody left to sell to,
- mints new tokens using the mint authority,
- freezes buyers' accounts, so nobody but the creator can sell.

<Callout type="risk" title="Memecoins are a lottery with worse odds">
  Treat money put into memecoins as spent the moment you send it. Most buyers
  lose, and even regular play with small amounts adds up. Never use leverage or
  savings for a memecoin.
</Callout>

## Common myths

- **"Whoever buys early wins."** Creators and bots are earlier than you. You are almost always "later".
- **"The token graduated, so it is safe."** Graduating only means it moved to an exchange.
- **"A famous name is behind the token, it cannot fall."** TRUMP and LIBRA showed the opposite.

<Callout title="Summary">

- Launchpads made it possible to create a token for a few cents; new tokens trade on a bonding curve.
- Only about 1% of tokens or fewer make it to an exchange, and most of those quickly lose value.
- Creators, bots and platforms make money; late buyers pay.
- Rug pull: the creator sells, removes liquidity, mints more or freezes accounts.

</Callout>
