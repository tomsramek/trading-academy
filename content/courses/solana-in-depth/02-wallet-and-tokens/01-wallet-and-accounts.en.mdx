On Solana everything is an account: your address, your tokens, every program and its data. Like a big
apartment block where every flat has a number and every tenant pays a deposit. Once you understand that,
you stop being surprised by the small amounts of SOL that "disappear" the first time you receive a new
token.

## Address and seed phrase

A Solana address is a long string of letters and digits (32 to 44 characters), such as `7xKX…9fQm`. It is
your public key – you can give it to anyone who wants to send you money.

The wallet is backed up by a [seed phrase](/courses/crypto-basics/wallets) of 12 or 24 words, just like on
other networks. Whoever knows it controls all your funds. Solana wallets exist as browser extensions,
mobile apps and hardware wallets – for example Phantom, Solflare or Ledger, mentioned only as examples.

## Every token has its own account

On Ethereum, your address has just a row in a table for each token. On Solana, every token in your wallet
has **its own account** – a so-called <KeyTerm term="token-account">token account</KeyTerm> owned by your
address. It is created the first time you receive a new token. Like a new mailbox in the apartment block
for each kind of letter.

## Account rent

Every account takes up space in the memory of all validators, so it has to hold a deposit in SOL. This is
called <KeyTerm term="account-rent">account rent</KeyTerm>, although it is really a refundable deposit:

- A token account needs about **0.002 SOL**. Whoever creates the account pays the deposit – usually the
  sender, or you yourself when buying.
- When you empty a token account and **close** it, the deposit comes back to you.
- The address itself must hold either zero or at least a small minimum (about 0.0009 SOL). A transaction
  that would leave less will fail.

Closing empty accounts is offered by wallets and by various websites – and scams often hide behind such
"clean-up services". Only clean up in a wallet you trust.

## Withdrawing from an exchange

- **Pick the right network.** Send SOL and Solana tokens over the Solana network. A token sent over a
  different network may not be recoverable at all.
- **Send a small amount first.** You verify the address and the network for the price of one fee.
- **Keep a little SOL.** Without SOL for fees you cannot even send the tokens you hold.

<Callout type="warning" title="Check the whole address">
  Scammers send tiny amounts from addresses whose beginning and end match those
  you usually transact with. If you then copy an address from your history, you
  send the money to them. Always take the address from the source, not from your
  history.
</Callout>

## Common myths

- **"0.002 SOL disappeared from my wallet."** Most likely a token account was created. Close it and the
  deposit comes back.
- **"A Solana seed phrase works in only one wallet."** The same phrase loads in other Solana wallets too –
  which is why you never type it anywhere unless you need to.
- **"If I have tokens, I can pay fees with them."** Fees are paid in SOL.

<Callout title="Summary">

- On Solana everything is an account; your address is a public key, the seed phrase controls it.
- Every token has its own token account in the wallet, with a refundable deposit of about 0.002 SOL.
- An empty token account can be closed and the deposit returned – only in a trusted wallet.
- Withdraw from an exchange over the Solana network, send a small amount first and keep SOL for fees.

</Callout>
