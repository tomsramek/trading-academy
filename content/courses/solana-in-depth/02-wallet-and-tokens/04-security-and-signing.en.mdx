On Solana, money is rarely stolen by breaking cryptography. It is stolen with a signature – yours. One
click on "Approve" on a fake website and within seconds the wallet is empty. And because blocks are
produced every fraction of a second, there is no time to change your mind.

## One signature, many instructions

A Solana transaction can contain several instructions at once: send SOL, send all tokens, close accounts,
change the owner. One signature approves them all. That is exactly what a <KeyTerm term="wallet-drainer">drainer</KeyTerm> exploits – a program that slips
you a transaction which takes everything valuable in one step.

Drainers spread through:

- fake "claim your airdrop" or "mint a new NFT" pages,
- hacked project accounts on social networks and Discord,
- search ads that look like a link to a well-known app,
- spam NFTs and tokens with a link right in their name.

## Read the transaction preview

Good wallets simulate the transaction before you sign and show what happens to your balances. Look for:

- **Outgoing amounts you do not expect.** You want to "claim" a token and the preview shows all your SOL
  leaving? Close the page.
- **Wallet warnings.** A message like "this transaction may be malicious" is not excessive caution.
- **A change of owner or authority.** A legitimate app almost never asks you to transfer ownership of an
  account.
- **The simulation failed.** If the wallet cannot compute a preview, do not sign blind.

Signing a message (such as "sign in with this wallet") does not move money. Signing a transaction does.
Always read which of the two a site is asking for.

## Fake airdrops

Real token giveaways – a so-called <KeyTerm term="airdrop">airdrop</KeyTerm> – do exist. Projects use them
to reward early users. But fake ones are far more common. Warning signs:

- you received an unsolicited token or NFT with a link,
- the site rushes you ("10 minutes left"),
- the site's address differs from the real one by a letter,
- it asks for your seed phrase – no real project ever does, ever.

## Split your wallets

1. **Vault** – a hardware wallet for savings. You do not connect it to unknown sites.
2. **Everyday wallet** – for known apps and smaller amounts.
3. **Experiment wallet** – for new projects and airdrops. Only as much as you can lose.

If a drainer then gets your experiment wallet, your savings stay safe.

<Callout type="risk" title="A signed transaction cannot be undone">
  There is no cancellation or refund on a blockchain. Money that leaves through
  a signed transaction is usually gone for good. If you are not sure what you
  are signing, do not sign.
</Callout>

## Common myths

- **"I have antivirus, nothing can happen to me."** A drainer is not a virus on your computer – it is a
  transaction you sign yourself.
- **"I only connected my wallet, I did not sign anything."** Connecting alone does not move money, but it
  is the first step. The next window may ask for a signature.
- **"The airdrop came straight into my wallet, so it is genuine."** Anyone can send a token to anyone.

<Callout title="Summary">

- One signature can approve many instructions at once – that is how drainers work.
- Read the preview before signing: unexpected outgoing amounts, warnings, a change of owner or a failed
  simulation = stop.
- Unsolicited tokens and NFTs with links are bait; no real project asks for your seed phrase.
- Split your wallets into a vault, an everyday wallet and an experiment wallet.

</Callout>
