Fees on Solana are usually so small you hardly notice them. Until everyone rushes for the same new token.
Then the motorway turns into a toll booth with a fast lane – and whoever does not pay for priority waits in
the queue.

<Illustration name="tollBooth" />

## The base fee

Every transaction pays **5,000 lamports per signature**, i.e. 0.000005 SOL. An ordinary transaction has
one signature. At a SOL price of 150 USD that is less than a tenth of a cent. Half is burned, half goes to
the validator.

## The priority fee

Every transaction states how much computing it needs – in so-called compute units. On top of that it can
offer a price per unit. The result is the <KeyTerm term="priority-fee">priority fee</KeyTerm>:

**priority fee = number of compute units × price per unit**

The leader validator that assembles the block favours transactions with higher offers. Wallets usually
set the priority fee themselves depending on how busy the network is.

## Local fee markets

Solana does not raise prices for the whole network at once. Usually only specific accounts are crowded –
say the liquidity pool of a new memecoin that thousands of bots are fighting over. Transactions that touch
those accounts pay a high priority fee. Meanwhile an ordinary SOL transfer goes through for almost
nothing.

## Why a transaction fails

- **It expired.** Every transaction refers to a recent block and is valid only for about 150 blocks – with the shorter slots from October 2026 that is roughly 40 seconds. If it
  does not make it into a block by then, it lapses.
- **It exceeded the slippage.** When swapping tokens you set how far the price may move. If it moves
  further, the transaction fails – and protects you by doing so.
- **The priority was too low.** In a rush, those who offered more get ahead of you.
- **It ran out of compute.** A complex transaction needed more units than it asked for.

Watch out: **a failed transaction that made it into a block pays the fee** – both base and priority. An
expired transaction that never made it into a block pays nothing.

<Callout type="warning" title="Priority is not a guarantee">
  Even a high priority fee does not guarantee you buy at the price you see. In a
  chase for a new token, bots will still get ahead of you – and you pay fees
  even for the transactions that fail.
</Callout>

## Common myths

- **"Fees on Solana are always negligible."** Base fees yes, priority fees in a rush no.
- **"A failed transaction is free."** If it made it into a block, you pay the fee.
- **"A higher fee = a better price."** A fee buys a place in line, not a price.

<Callout title="Summary">

- The base fee is 0.000005 SOL per signature; half is burned.
- Priority fee = compute units × offered price; it buys priority in the block.
- Only crowded accounts get more expensive, not the whole network.
- A transaction can expire or fail – a failed one in a block still pays the fee.

</Callout>
