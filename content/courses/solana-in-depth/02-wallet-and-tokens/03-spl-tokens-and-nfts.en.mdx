Creating a token on Solana takes a few minutes and costs less than lunch. That is why hundreds of
thousands of them appear every month. But every token has rules written into it – and a few of them decide
whether your tokens really belong to you.

<Illustration name="twoKeys" />

## The mint: a token's birth certificate

Tokens on Solana follow the <KeyTerm term="spl-token">SPL</KeyTerm> standard. Every token has one main
account – the so-called mint. It records:

- the **total number** of tokens in circulation,
- the **number of decimal places**,
- **who may mint more tokens**,
- **who may freeze token accounts**.

The mint address is the only reliable identity of a token. Everything else – name, ticker, logo – anyone
can set to whatever they like.

## The key to the printer and the key to the freezer

- **The printer.** The so-called <KeyTerm term="mint-authority">mint authority</KeyTerm> – whoever holds
  it can print new tokens. If a memecoin's creator keeps it, they can print a billion coins at any time
  and sell them against you.
- **The freezer.** The so-called <KeyTerm term="freeze-authority">freeze authority</KeyTerm> – whoever
  holds it can freeze your token account. You then see the tokens but cannot send or sell them. That is
  exactly how traps you can only buy into work.

Freezing on its own does not mean a scam. Big stablecoins like USDC have it because of the issuer's legal
obligations. For an unknown memecoin, though, it is a warning sign.

Newer versions of the standard (so-called token extensions) can also add, for example, a fee on every
transfer or a "permanent delegate" allowed to move or burn tokens from any account. Useful for companies,
dangerous in a scammer's hands.

## A name and a logo guarantee nothing

Anyone can create a token called "USDC" with the right logo. The real USDC on Solana has the mint:

```
EPjFWdd5AufqSSqeM2qN1xzybapC8G4wEGGkZwyTDt1v
```

Verify the mint address with the issuer or in a trusted
overview, not by the picture in your wallet.

## NFTs and spam NFTs

A so-called <KeyTerm term="nft">NFT</KeyTerm> is a token that exists as a single piece and has no decimal
places. Solana also supports so-called compressed NFTs, which cost a fraction of a cent to create. That is
why NFTs you never asked for often land in your wallet: "Congratulations, you won 5,000 USDC, claim them
at …"

That is bait. The link leads to a page that asks you to sign a transaction – and that transaction empties
your wallet. Ignore or hide spam NFTs and do not click their links. Signing is the topic of the next
lesson.

## Common myths

- **"The token has the right logo, so it is genuine."** Anyone can set a logo; the mint address decides.
- **"A freeze authority = always a scam."** It is common for regulated stablecoins; for an anonymous
  memecoin it is a warning.
- **"A free NFT in my wallet is a gift."** It is almost always bait.

<Callout title="Summary">

- A Solana token is defined by its mint: supply, decimals, mint and freeze authorities.
- The mint authority allows printing new tokens, the freeze authority blocking yours.
- The only reliable identity of a token is its mint address, not its name or logo.
- Unknown NFTs in your wallet are bait – do not click their links.

</Callout>
