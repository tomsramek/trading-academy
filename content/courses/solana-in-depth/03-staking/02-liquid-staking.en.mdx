Native staking has one downside: your SOL is locked and you wait days to get it back. Liquid staking
promises both – the staking yield and the option to sell at any time. It works like a theatre cloakroom:
you hand in your coat and get a ticket. And the ticket can be sold, lent or put somewhere else.

<Illustration name="coatCheck" />

## How it works

With <KeyTerm term="liquid-staking">liquid staking</KeyTerm> you put SOL into a stake pool (a protocol).
The pool spreads it across validators and gives you a **staking token** – on Solana, for example jitoSOL
or mSOL, mentioned only as examples.

The number of staking tokens you hold does not change. What rises is their **rate to SOL**, because the
pool keeps receiving rewards. Example: you deposit 10 SOL at a rate of 1 token = 1.20 SOL and get 8.33
tokens. A year later the rate might be 1.27 SOL, and your 8.33 tokens are worth 10.58 SOL. _Sample
numbers._

## The way back to SOL

- **Fast:** you sell the staking token for SOL on an exchange. Immediately, but at the market price –
  usually a little below fair value, and with slippage.
- **Cheap:** you ask the pool to unstake and wait until the end of the epoch. You get the fair value but
  wait just like with native staking.

## The extra risks

- **A bug in the contract.** The pool is a program. A bug or an attack can put all deposited SOL at risk.
- **A discount to fair value.** In a panic everyone wants out at once and the staking token sells at a
  discount on exchanges. It happened on Ethereum in June 2022: staked ETH traded about 6% below ETH. Whoever
  had to sell right away sold at a loss.
- **Pool fees.** The pool takes a percentage of the rewards – on top of the validators' commission.
- **Leverage on leverage.** A staking token can be posted as collateral, SOL borrowed against it, that SOL
  staked again, and so on. The yield multiplies – and so does the risk. When the staking token falls below
  fair value, liquidation can follow.
- **Concentration.** Big pools concentrate stake with selected validators. That may not be good for the
  network's decentralisation.

<Callout type="risk" title="Layers of yield = layers of risk">
  Every extra layer – a staking token, a loan, more staking – adds yield and
  another place where something can go wrong. A higher percentage on the screen
  does not mean you will get more. It means more can go wrong.
</Callout>

## Common myths

- **"A staking token is always worth SOL plus rewards."** On an exchange it can sell at a discount.
- **"Liquid staking is safer because I can sell any time."** You can sell, but not always at a fair price
  – and contract risk is added.
- **"A leveraged staking loop is free extra yield."** It is leverage with everything that comes with it.

<Callout title="Summary">

- Liquid staking: you put SOL into a pool and get a staking token whose rate to SOL rises.
- Back to SOL fast (sell on an exchange at a discount) or cheap (unstake and wait).
- Extra risks: contract bugs, discounts in a panic, pool fees, leverage and concentration.
- Every layer of yield adds a layer of risk.

</Callout>
