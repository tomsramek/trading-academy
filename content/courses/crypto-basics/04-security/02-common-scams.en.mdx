Scammers love crypto: payments are fast, irreversible and cross borders. In the US alone, according
to the FBI, people reported **11.4 billion dollars** lost to crypto scams in 2025. But the scams repeat
over and over – you just need to know them.

## Giveaways

"Send us 1 BTC and we'll send you 2 back!" – usually with a photo or a deepfake video of a celebrity
and a countdown.

<Illustration name="giveawayScam" />

Nobody gives away money by first asking you to send yours. **Ever.** What you send, you will not see
again.

## Fake support

You post on social media that your withdrawal is stuck, and within minutes "exchange support" writes
to you. They will help… if you tell them your seed phrase, install a "verification app" or send money
to a "safe address". Real support does not message you first and never wants sensitive details.

## Pump and dump

A group secretly buys a small, thinly traded coin. Then they whip up hype on social media – "this
coin will do 100×!" – and the price shoots up. Once enough new buyers have jumped in, the organisers
sell everything and the price collapses.

## Rug pull

The creators of a new token take the investors' money and vanish – they literally "pull the rug".
Often these are projects with an anonymous team, grand promises and pressure to buy as soon as
possible.

## Romance and friendship scams

The most expensive of all. The scammer chats with you for months as a friend or partner, wins your
trust and then "teaches you to invest" on their platform. The profits on the screen keep growing
until you want to withdraw. Then comes a "tax" or a "release fee" – and finally silence. This is
called _pig butchering_. Crypto investment fraud, most often of this kind, cost people in the US alone 7.2 billion dollars in 2025, according to the FBI.

<Callout type="risk" title="One rule for almost everything">
  If someone promises a sure profit, pushes you to decide quickly, or asks for
  your seed phrase, access to your computer or money upfront – it is a scam. No
  matter how trustworthy it looks.
</Callout>

## If it happens anyway

- **Do not pay anything more** – "crypto recovery" for a fee is just another scam.
- **Secure your accounts** – change passwords, check 2FA, move the remaining funds.
- **Report it** – to the police and, if a licensed firm was involved, to the regulator.

## Common myths

- **"It won't happen to me, I'm not stupid."** Scams target emotions – trust, fear, greed. Not
  intelligence.
- **"It's a verified account, so it's real."** Accounts get stolen and badges get bought.
- **"It's been paying out profits so far."** Small withdrawals at the start are part of the plan.

<Callout title="Summary">

- Giveaway: "send 1, get 2 back" is always a scam.
- Fake support messages first and asks for sensitive data; real support never does.
- Pump and dump and rug pulls: hype around a small coin, then a sharp fall.
- Sure profit + urgency + a request for money or your seed phrase = a scam.

</Callout>
