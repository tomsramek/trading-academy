Taxes are not the most fun part of crypto. But **forgotten records can cost more than a bad trade.**
This lesson is no substitute for a tax adviser – it shows what to think about, so you are not later
hunting for receipts from 2021.

<Illustration name="receiptPile" />

## Why keep records

For every purchase and sale, write down:

- **date and time**,
- **what and how much** you bought or sold,
- **the price in your currency** and **the fee**,
- **where** (exchange, wallet).

Without this you cannot work out whether you made or lost money – or prove how long you held the
coins. Most exchanges let you download your history as a file; download it regularly, since an
exchange can go bust or shut down.

## An example: taxes in the Czech Republic (since 2025)

Tax rules differ from country to country – check yours. As an example, since 15 February 2025 the
Czech Republic has **two tax exemptions** for income from selling crypto:

| Exemption    | Condition                                                                             |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Small income | total income from selling crypto in a year does not exceed **CZK 100,000**            |
| Holding test | **more than 3 years** passed between buying and selling (up to CZK 40 million a year) |

Two important details:

- CZK 100,000 is **income** (what you received from the sale), not profit. Exceed it by a single crown
  and all the income is taxed, not just the part above the limit.
- A "sale" usually also includes **swapping one cryptocurrency for another** or paying with crypto.

Rules change and every situation is a little different – for larger amounts, talk to a tax adviser.

## DAC8: exchanges report trades to the authorities

Since **1 January 2026**, the EU's <KeyTerm term="dac8">DAC8</KeyTerm> directive applies. Crypto-asset
service providers collect data about their customers and their trades and pass it to tax
authorities – the first exchange of data for 2026 takes place by the end of September 2027. In
practice: **the tax office will know about your exchange trades**, even if you never tell it.

<Callout type="warning" title="Don't wait until the last minute">
  Piecing together years of trades from several exchanges after the fact is a
  pain, and mistakes creep in easily. Record them as you go – a spreadsheet or a
  portfolio-tracking app is enough.
</Callout>

## Common myths

- **"Only what I send to my bank account is taxed."** Selling or swapping crypto can already create a
  tax obligation.
- **"The tax office won't find out."** With DAC8, it will.
- **"I don't need to record losses."** You need complete records – losses affect the result too.

<Callout title="Summary">

- Record every purchase and sale: date, amount, price, fee and place.
- Tax rules depend on your country; in the Czech Republic, yearly income up to CZK 100,000 and sales
  after more than 3 years of holding are exempt since 2025.
- Since 2026, exchanges in the EU report trades to tax authorities (DAC8).
- For larger amounts, talk to a tax adviser.

</Callout>
