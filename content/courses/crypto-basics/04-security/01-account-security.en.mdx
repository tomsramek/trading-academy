Crypto is rarely lost because hackers break the blockchain. It is lost because **people hand someone
the key to the door** – a password, a code or a seed phrase. The good news: three simple locks stop
most attacks.

## 1. Password: long, unique, in a manager

- **A different password for every service.** When a password leaks from an online shop, attackers
  immediately try it on exchanges.
- **Long rather than complex.** Four random words ("blue-sock-jumps-lamp") are stronger and easier to
  remember than "P@ssw0rd1".
- **A password manager** remembers them for you and will not fill them in on a fake site – that is
  protection too.
- **Protect your email the most.** Whoever controls your email can reset the passwords to everything
  else.

## 2. Two-factor authentication (2FA)

With <KeyTerm term="2fa">two-factor authentication</KeyTerm>, a password is not enough to log in –
you also need a one-time code. Where it comes from matters a lot:

| Method            | Security | Why                                                                                  |
| ----------------- | -------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| SMS               | lower    | an attacker can get a copy of your SIM card issued (SIM swap) and receive your texts |
| Authenticator app | good     | the code is created right on your phone, not in the carrier's network                |
| Hardware key      | best     | a physical key at your computer; phishing cannot fool it                             |

Print your 2FA backup codes and keep them as carefully as your seed phrase.

## 3. Phishing: don't bite the bait

<Illustration name="phishingHook" />

A <KeyTerm term="phishing">phishing</KeyTerm> message is a fake one pretending to be an exchange, a
bank or support, trying to make you click and enter your details. You can spot it by:

- **urgency** – "your account will be blocked within 24 hours", "suspicious login, verify now",
- **the address** – `binnance-support.com` instead of the real domain; read it letter by letter,
- **requests for sensitive data** – a password, a 2FA code, your seed phrase. **Nobody honest wants
  your seed phrase. Ever.**

**The rule:** do not click links in messages. Open the exchange's app or website yourself (from a
bookmark) and check whether anything is really going on.

<Callout type="warning" title="Support does not message you first">
  Real exchange support will not contact you via WhatsApp, Telegram or social
  media and never asks for your password, 2FA code or seed phrase. Whoever asks
  for them is a scammer – no exceptions.
</Callout>

## Other small things that help

- **An updated phone and computer** – security fixes.
- **A withdrawal address whitelist**, if the exchange offers one – money then only goes to addresses
  you approved in advance.
- **A PIN with your mobile carrier** against a new SIM card being issued.

## Common myths

- **"My password is complex, I don't need 2FA."** A password can leak elsewhere. 2FA is a second
  lock.
- **"An SMS code is good enough."** Better than nothing, but an authenticator app is safer.
- **"I'd always recognise phishing."** Good phishing looks exactly like the original. Hence the
  rule: do not click links in messages.

<Callout title="Summary">

- A different long password for every service, ideally from a password manager; protect your email
  the most.
- Turn on 2FA with an authenticator app or a hardware key, not SMS.
- Phishing lures with urgency and look-alike addresses – don't click, open the site yourself.
- Never give anyone your password, 2FA code or seed phrase.

</Callout>
