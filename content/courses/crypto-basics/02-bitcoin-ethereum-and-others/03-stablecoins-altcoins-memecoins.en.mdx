There are thousands of cryptocurrencies. Some have large teams and billion-dollar networks behind
them, others started as a joke about a dog. To begin with, it helps to sort them into three groups.

## Stablecoins: crypto that (mostly) stands still

A <KeyTerm term="stablecoin">stablecoin</KeyTerm> is a token whose price is meant to stay the same –
most often **1 dollar**. The best known include USDT and USDC. The issuer promises to hold a real
dollar or safe bonds in reserve for every token issued.

What is that good for? On exchanges, stablecoins are used as a "parking spot": you sell bitcoin for
USDT and do not have to send the money back to a bank. And you can send them anywhere in the world
within minutes.

The catch is trust: **a stablecoin is only as stable as its issuer is honest** and its reserves are
good. 2022 showed the worst case.

## The fall of Terra/LUNA: 99.99% in a week

The UST stablecoin did not hold dollars in reserve. Its price was kept at one dollar only by an
algorithm tied to a second cryptocurrency, LUNA. On 9 May 2022, UST lost its peg to the dollar and
a spiral began: people sold UST, the algorithm printed more LUNA, and LUNA fell faster and faster.

From **119.55 dollars** in early April, **0.00005 dollars** was left on 13 May. Then Binance ended
trading. A cryptocurrency that ranked among the largest in the world practically vanished in a few
days.

## Altcoins: everything else

The word <KeyTerm term="altcoin">altcoin</KeyTerm> is short for "alternative coin" – any
cryptocurrency other than Bitcoin. It covers big networks like Ethereum, but also thousands of small
projects promising to be "faster", "cheaper" or "revolutionary".

Most of them disappear within a few years or lose almost all their value. Before putting money into
one, ask:

- **Who is behind the project?** Real names, or an anonymous team?
- **What problem does it solve?** Does it do it better than what exists, or does it just promise?
- **Who holds most of the coins?** If it is the creators, they can sell any time.
- **Does it promise a sure profit?** Then run.

## Memecoins: a lottery with a dog

A <KeyTerm term="memecoin">memecoin</KeyTerm> is a cryptocurrency born from an internet joke. The
best known is **Dogecoin**, launched on 6 December 2013 as a parody featuring a Shiba Inu dog.
Memecoins usually solve nothing – their price is driven only by attention on social media.

<Illustration name="memeSlot" />

Memecoin prices can shoot up hundreds of percent in a day – and fall just as fast. It is more of a
lottery than an investment. And unlike a lottery, nobody checks whether the draw is rigged.

<Callout type="risk" title="The smaller the coin, the bigger the risk">
  Small altcoins and memecoins are easy to manipulate: a few big players buy in,
  stir up hype and sell to those who came late. Be prepared to lose all the
  money you put in.
</Callout>

## Common myths

- **"A coin at 0.0001 dollars is cheap, it can easily go up a hundred times."** The price of one coin
  tells you nothing. What matters is what all coins are worth together (market capitalization). If
  there are trillions of them, even a tiny price means a huge value – and a hundredfold rise is
  nearly impossible.
- **"A stablecoin is as safe as money in a bank."** It is not a bank deposit, and no deposit
  insurance covers it.
- **"If it is on a big exchange, it has been checked."** LUNA was on every big exchange.

<Callout title="Summary">

- Stablecoins keep their price around a dollar – only if the issuer is honest and has reserves.
- The algorithmic stablecoin UST, and LUNA with it, collapsed within days in May 2022.
- Altcoins are all cryptocurrencies other than Bitcoin; most of them do not survive.
- Memecoins started as jokes and are driven by hype alone – they are a lottery.

</Callout>
