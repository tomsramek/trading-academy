Bitcoin basically does one thing: send bitcoin. At the end of 2013, a nineteen-year-old programmer
called **Vitalik Buterin** asked: what if a blockchain could run **any program**? That is how
Ethereum was born – the network went live on **30 July 2015**.

## A smart contract: a vending machine without a shop assistant

Think of a vending machine. You put in a coin, choose a number and the machine hands you a snack.
No shop assistant, no haggling – the rules are built right into the machine.

<Illustration name="vendingMachine" />

A <KeyTerm term="smart-contract">smart contract</KeyTerm> works the same way, just on a blockchain:
it is a program with rules like "when a payment arrives, send back a token". Once uploaded to
Ethereum, it runs on its own, everyone can see it and nobody can quietly change it.

Running it costs a fee in Ethereum's currency (ETH), called <KeyTerm term="gas">gas</KeyTerm> –
literally the fuel. The more complex the program and the more people want to use the network at
that moment, the more fuel you burn.

## What has been built on Ethereum

- **<KeyTerm term="token">Tokens</KeyTerm>** – custom currencies or vouchers anyone can create with a smart contract. Most
  "cryptocurrencies" you have heard of are actually tokens on Ethereum or a similar network.
- **DeFi** (decentralized finance) – loans, exchanges or savings without a bank, run only by
  contracts. This is called <KeyTerm term="defi">DeFi</KeyTerm>.
- **NFTs** – a digital "certificate of ownership" for a picture, a ticket or an in-game item. In
  2021–2022 they caused a frenzy, and many sold for millions; a large share of them is worth almost
  nothing today.

## Bitcoin vs. Ethereum

|                   | Bitcoin                   | Ethereum                    |
| ----------------- | ------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Main purpose      | storing and sending value | a platform for programs     |
| Number of coins   | max. 21 million           | no fixed cap                |
| Who writes blocks | miners (proof of work)    | validators (proof of stake) |
| New block         | ~10 minutes               | ~12 seconds                 |

<Callout type="risk" title="Code does exactly what it says">
  Including its mistakes. If a smart contract has a bug, an attacker can exploit
  it and drain the money – and no customer hotline will bring it back. Huge sums
  have been lost in DeFi this way. Do not send money you are not willing to risk
  to contracts you do not know.
</Callout>

## Common myths

- **"Ethereum is just another Bitcoin."** Bitcoin is mainly money; Ethereum is mainly a platform
  that other things run on.
- **"A smart contract is smart."** It is just a program. It does not know what is fair – it does
  exactly what someone wrote. Even if that someone made a mistake.
- **"An NFT means I own the picture."** Usually you only own a record on the blockchain that points
  to the picture – it does not give you the copyright.

<Callout title="Summary">

- Ethereum (2015) is a blockchain that runs programs – smart contracts.
- A smart contract is like a vending machine: rules in code, no middleman.
- Tokens, DeFi and NFTs were built on Ethereum; running programs costs fees (gas).
- A bug in the code can mean lost money – nobody can "bend" the code, not even for a good reason.

</Callout>
