On 22 May 2010, programmer Laszlo Hanyecz ordered two pizzas and paid **10,000 bitcoins** for them.
Back then that was about 41 dollars. It was one of the first real purchases made with bitcoin – and
the day is still celebrated as _Bitcoin Pizza Day_.

<Illustration name="pizzaDay" />

Laszlo says he has no regrets. We might have a few. But how did "a few dollars for a pizza" turn
into more than 120,000 dollars for a single bitcoin in 2025?

## Only 21 million

Bitcoin's rules say **there will never be more than 21 million bitcoins**. New coins are only
created as the miners' reward for new blocks (you know that from the lesson on mining), and that
reward keeps getting smaller.

You do not have to buy a whole bitcoin, though. It splits into a hundred million pieces – the
smallest is called a <KeyTerm term="satoshi">satoshi</KeyTerm> (0.00000001 BTC), after Bitcoin's
creator. You can buy bitcoin for a few euros.

## The halving: half the reward

Roughly every four years (exactly every 210,000 blocks) the block reward is cut in half. This is
called the <KeyTerm term="halving">halving</KeyTerm>.

| Year | Block reward |
| ---- | ------------ |
| 2009 | 50 BTC       |
| 2012 | 25 BTC       |
| 2016 | 12.5 BTC     |
| 2020 | 6.25 BTC     |
| 2024 | 3.125 BTC    |

So new bitcoins arrive more and more slowly. The last one will be created around the year 2140 –
after that, miners will live on fees alone.

## Digital gold

There is a limited amount of gold in the world and nobody can "print" more. Bitcoin works in a
similar way, which is why it is nicknamed **digital gold**: people buy it to store value, not to
pay for their groceries.

There is a big difference, though: gold has thousands of years of history, Bitcoin seventeen. And its
price moves far more wildly.

<Callout type="warning" title="A halving does not guarantee a rise">
  After past halvings the price rose in the following months. That does not mean
  it always will – the market has long known and priced it in. The past
  guarantees nothing.
</Callout>

## Common myths

- **"I have to buy a whole bitcoin."** You do not – you can buy any fraction.
- **"Bitcoin is anonymous."** More like public: every payment stays visible on the blockchain
  forever.
- **"The price always rises after a halving."** See the box above – and the 77% drop in the chart.

<Callout title="Summary">

- There will never be more than 21 million bitcoins, and each splits down to satoshis.
- Every four years the block reward is cut in half (the halving); since 2024 it is 3.125 BTC.
- The limited supply earned it the nickname digital gold.
- The price can fall by three quarters – limited supply does not mean a stable price.

</Callout>
