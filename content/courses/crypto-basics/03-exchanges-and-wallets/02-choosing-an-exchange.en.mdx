it is far more useful to be able to choose yourself. And to start with a boring thing: the licence.

## The MiCA regulation: a licence is mandatory in the EU

The European Union adopted the <KeyTerm term="mica">MiCA</KeyTerm> regulation (Markets in
Crypto-Assets). Its rules for firms providing crypto services have applied since 30 December 2024.
The transition period ended on **1 July 2026** – since then, **only licensed firms** may offer
crypto-asset services to people in the EU. A licence from one EU country lets a firm operate across
the whole EU; in the Czech Republic, licences are granted and supervised by the **Czech National
Bank**.

What this means for you: before signing up, **check that the exchange has a licence**. The European
authority ESMA and national regulators publish lists of licensed firms.

## Checklist

- **A MiCA licence** – and from whom (your national regulator or one in another EU country).
- **Identity check (KYC)** – the exchange asks for your ID. It may be annoying, but it is a good
  sign: an honest exchange has to know who it is dealing with.
- **Fees** – for trades, the spread, deposits and withdrawals. Compare the total cost, not one number.
- **Deposits and withdrawals** – can you use a bank transfer in your currency? And is it easy to
  withdraw?
- **Security** – two-factor authentication (2FA), withdrawal confirmations, rules for new addresses.
- **Support and language** – when something goes wrong, you want to be understood.

## What happened to FTX

In 2022, FTX was one of the largest crypto exchanges in the world. It had famous ads, a sports arena
with its name on it and celebrity faces. **On 8 November 2022 it froze withdrawals**, and three days
later it went bankrupt. It turned out that customers' money had been secretly used by a sister
company – about **8 billion dollars** was missing from the accounts. Customers waited years to get
their money back.

The lesson: **size and advertising are no proof of honesty.** That is one reason regulation exists
today.

<Callout type="risk" title="A licence is not insurance">
  A licence means someone supervises the exchange – not that nothing can happen
  to you. Crypto on an exchange is not a bank deposit, and deposit insurance
  does not cover it. Keep only what you need for trading on an exchange.
</Callout>

## Red flags – walk away

- **It promises a sure or high return** ("1% a day", "no risk").
- **It has no licence**, or you cannot verify it anywhere.
- **It contacted you first** – via social media, WhatsApp or an "adviser" pushing you to decide fast.
- **You cannot withdraw**, or withdrawing requires another deposit or a "release fee".

## Common myths

- **"A big exchange cannot go bust."** FTX did.
- **"An identity check is suspicious."** Quite the opposite – an exchange that does not want one is
  suspicious.
- **"Lowest fee = best exchange."** Fees come after the licence and security.

<Callout title="Summary">

- Since 1 July 2026, only firms licensed under MiCA may offer crypto-asset services in the EU.
- In the Czech Republic, licences are granted by the Czech National Bank; check the licence before
  signing up.
- Look at identity checks, fees, withdrawals, security and support.
- A sure return, a missing licence or trouble withdrawing = walk away.
