You can think of an exchange as a vegetable market. Only instead of carrots it sells bitcoin,
nobody shouts and the stall never closes. The exchange itself sells nothing – **it matches people
who want to buy with people who want to sell**. And takes a small fee for it.

## The order book

All waiting offers are listed in the order book. Sellers and their prices are at the top, buyers at
the bottom:

<OrderBook />

Between the lowest price someone sells at and the highest price someone buys at there is a gap –
called the <KeyTerm term="spread">spread</KeyTerm>. The more people trade on the exchange, the
smaller the spread. Thinly traded coins often have a wide one, and it costs you money the moment you
buy.

## Order types

| Order  | When it fills                      | Price                | When to use it                   |
| ------ | ---------------------------------- | -------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Market | immediately                        | best available       | when speed matters               |
| Limit  | when the price reaches your limit  | your price or better | when the price matters           |
| Stop   | when the price hits the stop price | market               | to limit a loss (lesson on risk) |

A market order takes offers from the book one after another until you have bought everything you
want. With a big order or a thinly traded coin, part of it fills at worse prices. This difference is
called <KeyTerm term="slippage">slippage</KeyTerm>.

A limit order is more patient: it is written into the book and waits for someone to take it. It may
also never fill.

## What you pay for on an exchange

- **Trading fee** – usually a fraction of a percent of every purchase and sale. A limit order waiting
  in the book is often cheaper than a market order.
- **Spread** – a hidden fee, see above.
- **Deposits and withdrawals** – a bank transfer is usually cheap, a card payment pricier. When you
  withdraw crypto, you also pay the network fee.

<Callout type="warning" title="Coins on an exchange are not held by you">
  As long as your crypto sits on an exchange, technically the exchange holds it
  – you only have a record in your account. If the exchange goes bust or freezes
  withdrawals, you can lose everything. Why, and how to protect yourself, comes
  in the lesson on wallets.
</Callout>

## Common myths

- **"The exchange sells me the bitcoin."** Usually not – another user sells it to you, the exchange
  just matches you.
- **"A market order = the price I see."** Not always. With slippage you pay more.
- **"A 0% fee means free."** The exchange then usually earns on the spread.

<Callout title="Summary">

- An exchange matches buyers with sellers; offers sit in the order book.
- The gap between the buy and sell price is the spread – a hidden fee.
- A market order is fast, a limit order protects the price; big market orders suffer from slippage.
- You pay trading fees, the spread, and fees for deposits and withdrawals.

</Callout>
