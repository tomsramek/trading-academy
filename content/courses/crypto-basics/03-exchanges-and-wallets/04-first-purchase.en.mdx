This is a practical lesson. It is not about buying a lot – it is about **getting your first purchase
right**. Feel free to use an amount you would spend on a dinner. The steps are general and work on
any licensed exchange.

<Illustration name="firstBuy" />

## 1. Pick a licensed exchange

As in the previous lesson: a MiCA licence, checked fees and withdrawals. Download the app **only from
the exchange's official website or the official app store** – scammers love making fake copies.

## 2. Sign up and verify your identity

Enter your email, a strong password (ideally from a password manager) and go through the identity
check – upload an ID and a selfie. Verification can take anything from minutes to a few days.

## 3. Turn on two-factor authentication

Before sending any money, turn on **2FA with an authenticator app** (not SMS, if you have a choice).
Details in the Security module.

## 4. Send a small amount to the exchange

A **bank transfer** in your currency, following the exchange's instructions, is usually cheapest.
A card payment is faster but pricier. Start with an amount you **can lose without stress**.

## 5. Buy

- Pick a cryptocurrency you understand (beginners mostly stick to Bitcoin or Ethereum – this is not a recommendation, both can fall sharply).
- For a small amount, a **market order** is fine; if you want to control the price, use a **limit
  order**.
- Before confirming, check the **total cost including the fee**.

## 6. Decide where to keep the coins

- **A small amount you want to sell soon** → it can stay on the exchange.
- **Long-term savings** → your own wallet. For the first transfer, send a **small test amount**
  first, check it arrived, and only then the rest. Check the address – at least its beginning and
  end.

## 7. Write it down

Date, amount, price, fee. In a year you will be glad you did – mainly for taxes (more in the lesson
on records).

<Callout type="risk" title="Don't rush">
  Whoever pushes you ("today only", "last chance") is usually thinking about
  their profit, not yours. Crypto is risky – only buy what you can afford to
  lose, and never on credit.
</Callout>

## The most common beginner mistakes

- **Too big a first amount** – nerves then make the decisions for you.
- **A fake app or website** from an ad or a link in a message.
- **The wrong network or address when withdrawing** – coins sent on the wrong network often cannot
  be recovered.
- **A seed phrase stored on the phone** – see the previous lesson.

<Callout title="Summary">

- A licensed exchange, the official app, identity check, 2FA turned on.
- A small first deposit, ideally by bank transfer; check the total cost before buying.
- Move savings to your own wallet – a test amount first.
- Write down every purchase, and never buy under pressure or on credit.

</Callout>
