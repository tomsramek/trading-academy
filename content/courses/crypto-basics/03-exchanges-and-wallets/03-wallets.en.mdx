A crypto <KeyTerm term="wallet">wallet</KeyTerm> does **not actually contain** coins. Coins are recorded on the blockchain. The
wallet only holds the **keys** that can move them. Whoever has the keys has the coins – whether it
is you or not.

<WalletKeys />

## On an exchange, or your own?

- **A wallet on an exchange** (the exchange manages the keys) – convenient, a forgotten password can
  be reset. But the exchange holds the keys. If it goes bust like FTX, you are out of luck.
- **Your own wallet** (you hold the keys) – nobody can freeze or embezzle your coins. But nobody can
  help you either if you lose the keys.

That is why crypto people say: **"Not your keys, not your coins."**

## Hot or cold?

|                     | <KeyTerm term="hot-wallet">Hot wallet</KeyTerm> | <KeyTerm term="cold-wallet">Cold wallet</KeyTerm> |
| ------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| What it is          | an app on your phone or computer                | a separate device (hardware wallet)               |
| Internet connection | always                                          | only when signing a payment                       |
| Convenience         | high                                            | lower                                             |
| Security            | lower – an attacker can get into the device     | higher                                            |
| For                 | smaller amounts for everyday use                | long-term savings                                 |

## The seed phrase: the master key

When you create your own wallet, you get **12 or 24 words** in an exact order – the so-called <KeyTerm term="seed-phrase">seed phrase</KeyTerm>. With it, the wallet can be
restored any time, anywhere. Whoever knows it controls all your coins. Whoever
loses it loses the coins forever.

<Illustration name="stickyNote" />

**How to handle a seed phrase:**

- write it **by hand on paper** (or stamp it into metal) and store it somewhere safe, ideally in two
  places,
- **never photograph it, copy it to a computer, email it** or store it in the cloud,
- **never read it out to anyone** – no support team, exchange or "technician" ever needs it,
- **never enter it on a website** someone sent you.

## What happens when you lose the keys

In 2013, a Briton called James Howells threw out an old hard drive while tidying up. It held the
key to **8,000 bitcoins**. The drive ended up in a landfill in Newport and James has been searching
for it ever since – in January 2025 a court dismissed his case against the city. Probably the most
expensive office clean-up in history.

<Callout type="warning" title="Nobody can help you">
  With your own wallet there is no "forgot password". Without the seed phrase
  the coins are lost forever – and with a stolen seed phrase a thief empties the
  wallet within minutes.
</Callout>

## Common myths

- **"My coins are in my wallet."** The coins are on the blockchain; the wallet only has the keys.
- **"A photo of my seed phrase is a backup."** It is an invitation for thieves.
- **"A hardware wallet means I can't lose anything."** You can still lose the seed phrase or sign a
  fraudulent payment.

<Callout title="Summary">

- A wallet holds keys, not coins; whoever has the keys has the coins.
- An exchange is convenient but holds the keys; your own wallet gives you control and
  responsibility.
- A hot wallet for smaller amounts, a cold one for savings.
- Write the seed phrase by hand on paper, keep it offline and never give it to anyone.

</Callout>
