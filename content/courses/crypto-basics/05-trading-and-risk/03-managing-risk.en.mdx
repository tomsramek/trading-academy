Successful traders are not the ones who always get the direction right. They are the ones who
**survive their mistakes**. You can be right half the time and still make money – if the losses are
small and the profits bigger. All of risk management revolves around one question: **how much do I
lose if I am wrong?**

## The 1% rule

Risk only a small part of your money on one trade – **1–2%** is often quoted. With an account of
1,000 euros and 1% risk, that is **10 euros**. Ten losing trades in a row will then not ruin you –
you lose about a tenth, not everything.

## Stop-loss: decide before you buy

A <KeyTerm term="stop-loss">stop-loss</KeyTerm> is an order that automatically sells your position
when the price falls to a level you chose in advance. You set it **before buying**, with a clear
head – not while the price is falling and you hope it will "bounce back".

<Illustration name="parachuteStop" />

The stop-loss goes where your assumption would turn out wrong – typically just below support.

## An example on a real chart

In early May 2024, Bitcoin touched support around 56,500. Imagine buying at **58,400**, with a
stop-loss below support at **55,500** and a profit target (<KeyTerm term="take-profit">take-profit</KeyTerm>) at **67,000**:

**How much to buy?** The stop-loss is 5% below the purchase price (58,400 → 55,500). You want to risk
10 euros, so position size = 10 euros ÷ 5% = **200 euros**. If the price falls to the stop-loss, you
lose 10 euros.

**Reward-to-risk ratio:** you risk 2,900 USDT per bitcoin (58,400 → 55,500), and the target is 8,600
USDT above the purchase. That is about **1 : 3** – you make three times more than you risk. Thanks to that,
you can be profitable even if only every third trade works out.

## Leverage: a fast road to zero

Trading with <KeyTerm term="leverage">leverage</KeyTerm> means trading with borrowed money. With 10× leverage, a 10%
price move against you wipes out your whole deposit. According to the EU's ESMA, **74–89% of retail
investor accounts** lose money trading leveraged CFDs. That is why the EU limited leverage on crypto
CFDs for retail investors to 2 : 1.

<Callout type="risk" title="A beginner does not need leverage">
  Leverage magnifies both profits and losses – and losses come faster than you
  can react. Until you have long experience with small amounts and no leverage,
  leave it alone.
</Callout>

## Common myths

- **"I'll set a stop-loss when I need one."** When you need one, emotions will decide.
- **"I'll move the stop-loss lower, it'll come back."** That is how big losses happen.
- **"A bigger position = a bigger profit."** And a bigger loss. Position size is driven by risk, not
  greed.

<Callout title="Summary">

- Risk only 1–2% of your account on one trade.
- Set the stop-loss before buying, where your assumption would turn out wrong.
- Position size = the amount you want to risk ÷ the distance to the stop-loss.
- Look for trades where the possible profit is bigger than the risk; as a beginner, avoid leverage.

</Callout>
