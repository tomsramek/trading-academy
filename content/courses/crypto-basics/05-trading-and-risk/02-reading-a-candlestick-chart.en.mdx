A chart is a trader's most common tool. It does not tell the future – it shows **what happened** and
how people behaved. If you can read it, at least you know where you are.

## A candle in a nutshell

Each <KeyTerm term="candlestick">candle</KeyTerm> sums up one period with four prices: open, high, low and close.

## Timeframe

The same market looks completely different depending on how long a period one candle covers. On a
one-minute chart it is a wild rollercoaster, on a weekly chart a calm road. This is called the <KeyTerm term="timeframe">timeframe</KeyTerm>.

- **Short timeframes** (minutes, hours) – lots of noise, quick decisions, easy to get caught out.
- **Long timeframes** (days, weeks) – less noise, a clearer big picture.

Beginners do well to start with the **daily chart** and also look at the weekly one, to know where
they are in the bigger picture.

## Trend

<Illustration name="candleSurfer" />

A <KeyTerm term="trend">trend</KeyTerm> is the direction the price moves in over a longer time:

- **uptrend** – every high and every low is above the previous one,
- **downtrend** – every high and low is lower,
- **sideways** – the price stays in a range between <KeyTerm term="support">support</KeyTerm> and <KeyTerm term="resistance">resistance</KeyTerm>.

## Candlestick patterns

Some candle shapes can hint at a turn – like the hammer or the engulfing pattern you saw in the
module on Bitcoin and the rest:

<CandlePattern name="bullishEngulfing" />

But patterns **guarantee nothing on their own**. At best they work as a warning that "something is
going on here", never as a sure signal to buy.

<Callout type="warning" title="A chart does not tell the future">
  Everything you see on a chart has already happened. A trend can end at any
  moment and support can break. Chart analysis improves the odds of a sensible
  decision – it does not guarantee a profit.
</Callout>

## Common myths

- **"I'll see more on a one-minute chart."** You will mostly see more noise.
- **"A hammer = the price will rise."** Maybe. Or not. That is what risk management is for.
- **"A trend lasts forever."** Every trend ends – the only question is when.

<Callout title="Summary">

- A candle shows the open, high, low and close of one period.
- The timeframe changes the picture: a longer timeframe = less noise.
- You recognise a trend by higher (or lower) highs and lows; it can end at any moment.
- Patterns are warnings, not certainty – a chart shows the past.

</Callout>
