A market is like a marketplace. Someone puts goods on the counter and waits for a customer – that is the
**maker**. Someone comes and takes the goods straight off the counter – that is the **taker**. The exchange
likes a full marketplace, so it rewards those who put goods out with a lower fee.

<Illustration name="makerTaker" />

## Maker or taker?

- **Maker** – your order does not fill straight away and waits in the order book. Typically a limit
  order below the current price when buying, or above it when selling. You add <KeyTerm term="liquidity">liquidity</KeyTerm> to the market.
- **Taker** – your order fills immediately against orders already in the book. A market order, and a
  limit order at a price that can be filled right away. You take liquidity.

All the costs of a trade are covered in the lesson
[What a trade costs](/courses/trading-with-a-plan/what-a-trade-costs).

## How to read the price list

The price list (**Fees** or **Fee schedule**) is usually a table by volume over the last 30 days:

| 30-day volume   | Maker | Taker |
| --------------- | ----- | ----- |
| up to 10,000 €  | 0.10% | 0.20% |
| 10,000–50,000 € | 0.08% | 0.16% |
| over 50,000 €   | 0.06% | 0.12% |

_A sample table – real rates differ by exchange._

At these rates a 1,000 € trade costs you 1 € as a maker and 2 € as a taker. You pay the fee again both
when buying and when selling.

Some exchanges offer a discount on fees if you pay them with their own token. The discount is nice, but
you have to hold the token – and its price may fall by more than you save.

## The withdrawal fee

Besides trading fees, you pay for withdrawing crypto from the exchange. It is usually a **fixed amount**
depending on the network, not a percentage. A fixed fee worth, say, 2 euros is 10% of a 20-euro
withdrawal – but only 0.1% of a 2,000-euro one. So withdraw less often and in larger amounts.

<Callout type="warning" title="The most expensive trade is an unnecessary one">
  A lower fee tempts you to trade more. But ten unnecessary trades with a low
  fee cost more than one well-thought-out trade with a higher one. The cheapest
  trade is the one you do not make.
</Callout>

## Common myths

- **"A limit order is always a maker."** Only if it does not fill straight away. A limit buy above the
  current price is a taker.
- **"0.1% is negligible."** It adds up with frequent trading – and you pay it on both buying and selling.
- **"Withdrawing a small amount is free if the fee is small."** A fixed fee is highest in percentage terms
  on small amounts.

<Callout title="Summary">

- A maker adds an order to the book and pays less; a taker takes from the book and pays more.
- The price list follows 30-day volume; you pay the fee on both buying and selling.
- A discount for paying with the exchange's own token means holding another risky asset.
- The withdrawal fee is usually fixed – withdraw less often and in larger amounts.

</Callout>
