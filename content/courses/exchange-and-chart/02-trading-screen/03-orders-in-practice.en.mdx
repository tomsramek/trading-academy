## A limit order: "I will buy when it is at that price"

Bitcoin costs 60,000 euros and you want to buy when it drops to 58,000.

1. The **Spot** tab, the **Buy** button, the **Limit** type.
2. **Price:** 58,000 – the highest price you are willing to pay.
3. **Amount:** 0.002 BTC – or fill in **Total:** 116 EUR. The fields are linked: price × amount = total.
4. Check the summary and confirm.

The order appears in **open orders** and waits. If the price never reaches 58,000, it never executes. If
you no longer want it, cancel it.

## A market order: "I will buy now, at whatever it goes for"

The **Market** type has no price field. You enter only an amount or a sum and buy immediately at the best
offers in the order book. It is fast, but you do not know the exact price in advance – especially with
thinly traded pairs and large amounts, <KeyTerm term="slippage">slippage</KeyTerm> can cost you.

## Stop-limit: "sell if it is falling"

You bought bitcoin at 60,000 and want to sell it if the price falls below 55,000.

1. **Sell**, the **Stop-limit** type.
2. **Stop (trigger price):** 55,000 – once a trade happens at this price, the order activates.
3. **Limit:** 54,500 – the lowest price you are willing to sell at. Leave some room between the stop and
   the limit, or the order may not execute in a sharp fall.
4. **Amount:** how much you are selling.

## The mistakes that cost the most

- **An extra or a missing zero.** 5,800 instead of 58,000 on a sale means selling at a tenth of the price.
  A limit sell below the current price executes immediately at the best bids.
- **Buy instead of Sell.** The buttons are side by side and differ only in colour.
- **The slider at 100%.** One drag bets your whole balance.
- **Futures instead of Spot.** See lesson 13.

<Callout type="warning" title="The preview before confirming">
  Most exchanges show a summary before sending: price, amount, total and fee.
  Read it. It is the last moment a mistake can be fixed for free.
</Callout>

## Common myths

- **"A limit buy above the current price will wait."** It does not execute at your higher price but
  immediately at the best offers in the book – which can still cost you more than you intended.
- **"A stop-limit always protects me."** In a sharp fall through your limit, the sale does not execute.
- **"A market order is the cheapest because it is instant."** You pay a higher fee and possibly slippage.

<Callout title="Summary">

- Limit: you enter price and amount; the order waits in open orders.
- Market: you enter only the amount; it executes now at the best offers – with slippage.
- Stop-limit: a trigger price plus a limit with room for a sharp move.
- Read the summary before confirming – an extra zero or mixing up Buy/Sell is the most expensive mistake.

</Callout>
