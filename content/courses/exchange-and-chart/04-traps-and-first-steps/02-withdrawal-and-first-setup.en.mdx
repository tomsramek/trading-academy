## Withdrawal step by step

1. **In your wallet, display a receiving address** – with a hardware wallet, verify it on its screen.
2. **On the exchange: Wallet → Withdraw**, choose the cryptocurrency.
3. **Choose the network.** It must be one your wallet supports. Send bitcoin over the Bitcoin network,
   not another network using the same ticker. Wrong network = the coins may not be recoverable.
4. **Paste the address** and check all of it, the middle too. If you use a withdrawal whitelist, pick it
   from there.
5. **Enter the amount.** The exchange shows the network fee and the minimum withdrawal.
6. **Confirm** with your password, 2FA and possibly an email code.
7. **Track the transaction.** The exchange shows its number (TXID). With it you can find the transaction
   in a block explorer and see how many confirmations it has.

Send a small amount first. Once it arrives, send the rest.

## Why the exchange asks whose address it is

Under European rules exchanges must find out who receives a transfer. So when you withdraw to your own
wallet, it asks whether the address is yours, and for larger amounts it may want proof – for example a
message signed in your wallet or a small verification payment as the app instructs. This is normal, and
the exchange does it directly in its app, not an "agent" in a chat.

## Your first setup

**Account**

- The exchange has a MiCA licence (checked in the national or ESMA register).
- A strong unique password, 2FA via a passkey or an app.
- An anti-phishing code and a withdrawal whitelist.

**Trading**

- Deposits by transfer from an account in your name, with the reference code.
- You buy on the **Spot** tab with a limit order and read the summary before confirming.
- You leave futures, margin, Earn and bonuses alone for now.

**Chart**

- A daily or four-hour chart, volume, one or two averages and RSI.
- Important levels drawn, with price alerts on them.

**Custody**

- Whatever you do not want to lose, you withdraw to your own wallet – first with a small test amount.
