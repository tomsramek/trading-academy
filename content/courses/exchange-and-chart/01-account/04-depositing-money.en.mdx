Depositing money is the most boring part of trading – and yet you can lose more on it than on your first
trade. Money can flow through a wide pipe for free, or through a narrow pipe with a toll booth.

<Illustration name="depositPipes" />

## Ways to deposit

| Method                               | Speed                                              | Typical cost                      |
| ------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| bank transfer in euros (SEPA)        | within one working day, instant payment in seconds | often free or a few tens of cents |
| bank transfer in your local currency | depends on the exchange                            | free to a small fee               |
| payment card                         | immediate                                          | usually 1.5–3.5% of the amount    |

_Indicative ranges – the exact price list is in the Fees section of the exchange's website._

A bank transfer in euros is called <KeyTerm term="sepa">SEPA</KeyTerm>. If your bank supports it as an
instant payment, the money is on the exchange within seconds.

## Where money gets lost

If your money is in another currency and the exchange only accepts euros, something has to convert it:

- **Your bank, when transferring** – the rate is often 1–2% worse than the mid-market rate.
- **A card** – on top of the exchange's fee there may be the card issuer's currency conversion.
- **An exchange with a local-currency account** – some exchanges accept your currency directly; the
  conversion then happens only when you buy.

Example for a 200 € deposit:

| Route                                       | Loss (roughly) |
| ------------------------------------------- | -------------- |
| card with a 2.5% fee                        | 5 €            |
| local currency converted by the bank (1.5%) | 3 €            |
| transfer from a euro account, free SEPA     | 0 €            |

_Sample numbers._

## Three mistakes that make money "disappear"

1. **Someone else's account.** The money must come from an account in your name. The exchange returns –
   or blocks – a payment from a friend or partner.
2. **A missing reference code.** The exchange shows you a code to put in the message for the recipient.
   Without it the exchange does not know whose money it is, and tracing it takes days.
3. **Wrong recipient details.** Copy the exchange's account number (IBAN) straight from its app, not from
   an email "someone sent you".

<Callout type="warning" title="A small test payment">
  For your first deposit, send a small amount. Once it arrives, you know the
  details and the reference code are right, and you can send the rest.
</Callout>

## Common myths

- **"Card is faster, so why not."** You may pay 2–3% for the speed – more than the fees on many trades.
- **"I will send it from my partner's account, it is shared anyway."** The exchange needs a payment from
  an account in the exchange account holder's name.
- **"The reference code is optional."** Without it, the money gets stuck.

<Callout title="Summary">

- A SEPA bank transfer in euros is usually cheapest; a card is fast but usually costs 1.5–3.5%.
- Converting currencies loses 1–2% in the rate – look for the cheapest route.
- Pay from an account in your name and do not forget the reference code.
- Send a small test payment first.

</Callout>
