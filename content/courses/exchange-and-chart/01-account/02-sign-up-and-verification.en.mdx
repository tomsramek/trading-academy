Signing up for an exchange is like opening a bank account on your phone: email, password, ID card,
selfie and a few questions. It can feel like an interrogation. In reality the exchange is just meeting
its legal duties – and you are on the other side, so it helps to know what is normal.

<Illustration name="idSelfie" />

## Step by step

1. **Email and password.** A long, unique password, ideally from a password manager. Reusing a password
   is an invitation to thieves – more in the
   [lesson on account security](/courses/crypto-basics/account-security).
2. **Phone number.** For contact and account recovery.
3. **Personal details.** Name, date of birth, address, citizenship.
4. **An ID document.** An ID card or passport – you photograph both sides in the app.
5. **A selfie or short video.** The app asks you to turn your head or blink. It checks that you are a
   living person and that the document is yours.
6. **A questionnaire.** Your job, where your money comes from, roughly how much you plan to invest.

Verification usually takes a few minutes, sometimes several days.

## Why all this

The process is called <KeyTerm term="kyc">KYC</KeyTerm> (know your customer). Licensed exchanges are
required by law to verify who their customers are and to watch for money laundering. So questions about
the source of your money are normal – just like at a bank. For larger deposits an exchange may want
proof, such as a bank statement or a contract for selling a property.

## What is not normal

| The exchange wants…                                          | Normal?                                 |
| ------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------- |
| a photo of your ID and a selfie in its app or on its website | yes                                     |
| questions about your job and the source of your money        | yes                                     |
| your documents sent via WhatsApp or Telegram to an "agent"   | **no – a scam**                         |
| a fee for "account activation" or "unblocking"               | **no – a scam**                         |
| crypto sent to a "verification" address                      | **no – a scam**                         |
| your seed phrase, password or 2FA code                       | **no – never, under any circumstances** |

<Callout type="warning" title="Documents only to the exchange itself">
  Upload documents only in the official app or on the exchange's website at an
  address you know. Scammers pose as "verification services" and collect ID
  photos to open accounts in your name.
</Callout>

## Common myths

- **"I do not want to hand over documents, I will find an exchange without verification."** An exchange
  without verification has no licence in the EU – and no supervision either.
- **"Questions about the source of money are harassment."** They are a legal duty; an honest exchange
  always asks.
- **"Support messaged me on Telegram to finish verification with them."** Support does not contact you
  on Telegram on its own.

<Callout title="Summary">

- Sign-up: email, a strong unique password, phone, personal details, an ID document, a selfie and a
  questionnaire.
- Verification (KYC) is a legal duty of licensed exchanges, just like at a bank.
- Upload documents only in the exchange's official app or website.
- An activation fee, a "verification" payment or a request for your seed phrase = a scam.

</Callout>
