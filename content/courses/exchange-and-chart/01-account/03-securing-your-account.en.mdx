You know the general security rules from [Crypto basics](/courses/crypto-basics/account-security). Here
is the practical version: open **Profile → Security** on your exchange (the name differs only slightly
between exchanges) and go through five switches with us. It takes a quarter of an hour and turns your
account from a tent into a fortress.

<Illustration name="securityToggles" />

## 1. Two-factor authentication

Besides your password, you need a second proof – a code or a confirmation.

- **Best:** a <KeyTerm term="passkey">passkey</KeyTerm> or a security key. It cannot be intercepted or
  phished on a fake website.
- **Good:** an authenticator app that generates six-digit codes.
- **Emergency only:** SMS. Better than nothing, but a scammer can move your number to another SIM card.
  Do not keep it as your only method.

## 2. The anti-phishing code

The exchange lets you set your own word – say "bluepenguin". The so-called <KeyTerm term="anti-phishing-code">anti-phishing code</KeyTerm> is then included in every email the
exchange sends you. **An email without your code does not come from the exchange**, however convincing it
looks.

## 3. Withdrawal whitelist

A <KeyTerm term="withdrawal-whitelist">withdrawal whitelist</KeyTerm> holds only addresses you have
approved yourself – your hardware wallet, for example. If an attacker gets into your account, they cannot
send the money to their own address. A new address usually becomes usable only after a waiting period,
often 24 hours.

## 4. Devices and logins

The device list shows where logins came from. Log out any unknown device and change your password. Turn
on alerts for every new login.

## 5. The account's email

Whoever controls your email can reset your exchange password. So secure the email your exchange account
uses just as well: a strong password and two-factor authentication.

<Callout type="warning" title="Changing security locks withdrawals for a while">
  When you change your password, 2FA or email, the exchange usually blocks
  withdrawals for 24–48 hours. It is not a bug but protection: if an attacker
  made the change, you have time to step in.
</Callout>

## Common myths

- **"An SMS code is enough."** Not as the only method – a SIM card can be moved.
- **"The email has the exchange's logo, so it is genuine."** Anyone can copy a logo. Your anti-phishing
  code decides.
- **"A withdrawal whitelist is a pointless hassle."** It is the cheapest insurance against your account
  being emptied.

<Callout title="Summary">

- Two-factor authentication: a passkey or an app, SMS only as a backup.
- An anti-phishing code tells genuine emails from the exchange apart from fakes.
- A withdrawal whitelist stops withdrawals to someone else's address.
- Watch the logged-in devices and secure the account's email too.

</Callout>
