## Timeframes

The so-called <KeyTerm term="timeframe">timeframe</KeyTerm> says how long a period one candle summarises:

| Abbreviation | One candle = |
| ------------ | ------------ |
| 1m           | 1 minute     |
| 15m          | 15 minutes   |
| 1H (1h)      | 1 hour       |
| 4H           | 4 hours      |
| 1D           | 1 day        |
| 1W           | 1 week       |
| 1M           | 1 month      |

On Binance a row of timeframes sits right above the chart, and more are in the arrow next to them:

Watch out for **1m and 1M**: a lowercase m is a minute, an uppercase M a month. There is a factor of
roughly 43,000 between them.

## The figures above the chart

When you hover over a candle, you see:

- **O** (open) – the candle's opening price,
- **H** (high) – the highest price,
- **L** (low) – the lowest price,
- **C** (close) – the closing price,
- **Vol** – volume, how much was traded,
- **Change** – how many percent the close is away from the open.

The values after indicator names, such as **MA(7): 67,210**, show where that line is at that moment.
Indicators have the next lesson.

## When the daily candle closes

Exchanges usually count the day in world time, UTC. So the daily candle closes at **midnight UTC** – in
Central Europe that is 1:00 in winter and 2:00 in summer, not at local midnight.

## The switches in the chart's corner

- **log** – logarithmic scale: the same distance on the axis is the same percentage change. Useful for long
  charts where the price grew many times over.
- **%** – the axis in percent from the start of the visible period.
- **auto** – the chart fits itself to the height of the visible candles.

<Callout type="warning" title="Shorter timeframe, more noise">
  On minute charts the price twitches constantly and every move looks important.
  Beginners do better with daily and four-hour charts – fewer signals, fewer
  emotions, fewer unnecessary trades.
</Callout>

## Common myths

- **"1m is a month."** A lowercase m is a minute; a month is an uppercase M.
- **"The daily candle closes at midnight."** Usually in UTC, which is not your local midnight.
- **"The hourly chart is more precise, so it is better."** It is more detailed, not better – and you see
  more noise in it.

<Callout title="Summary">

- Timeframe = the length of one candle; lowercase m is a minute, uppercase M a month.
- O, H, L, C are the open, high, low and close; Vol is volume.
- The daily candle usually closes by UTC.
- Start with a longer timeframe for direction; log and % in the corner change the axis scale.

</Callout>
