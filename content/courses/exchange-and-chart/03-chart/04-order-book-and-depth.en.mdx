The last price in the header is just the last trade. What you pay is decided by the order book: a list of
everyone who is waiting right now for someone to buy from them or sell to them.

<Illustration name="depthMountains" />

## What the book looks like

| Side           | Price (EUR) | Amount (BTC) |
| -------------- | ----------- | ------------ |
| sell           | 60,050      | 3.0          |
| sell           | 60,020      | 1.2          |
| sell           | 60,010      | 0.5          |
| **last price** | **60,000**  |              |
| buy            | 59,990      | 0.8          |
| buy            | 59,980      | 1.5          |
| buy            | 59,950      | 2.5          |

_A sample book – a real one is much longer._

- **Red rows at the top** – sellers and the least they will sell for. The lowest of them (60,010) is the
  price you buy at right away.
- **Green rows at the bottom** – buyers and the most they will pay. The highest (59,990) is the price you
  sell at right away.
- The difference between them is the <KeyTerm term="spread">spread</KeyTerm> – 20 euros here. The more
  liquid the market, the smaller the spread.

The icons above the book switch the view: both sides, buys only, sells only. The number on the right (0.01)
groups prices – with a step of 10 you see a few larger levels instead of hundreds of tiny rows.

## What a market order pays

A real order book on Binance:

<Figure
  src={book}
  alt="The Binance order book for BTC/USDT: red sell offers at the top with price, amount and total, the last price of 81,052.05 in the middle, green buy offers at the bottom and the buy/sell ratio below them"
  caption="Binance, BTC/USDT order book (8 Oct 2026): red sells at the top, green buys at the bottom, the last price in the middle. The 0.01 rounding choice is at the top right."
/>

A market buy takes the offers from the bottom of the red part until it has the whole amount:

| Purchase | Average price | Per BTC vs the last price |
| -------- | ------------- | ------------------------- |
| 0.1 BTC  | 60,010        | +10 €                     |
| 1 BTC    | 60,015        | +15 €                     |
| 2 BTC    | 60,022        | +22 €                     |

The bigger the purchase, the deeper it reaches into the book and the more it pays – that is <KeyTerm term="slippage">slippage</KeyTerm>. For bitcoin on a big exchange it is negligible. For small
altcoins with a thin book, one purchase can move the price by several percent.

## The depth chart

The **Depth** switch shows the book as two mountains: green on the left (buys) and red on the right
(sells). The height of a mountain is the total of all offers up to that price – the so-called <KeyTerm term="market-depth">market depth</KeyTerm>. A steep mountain means many offers near the price;
the gap in the middle is the spread.

<Callout type="warning" title="Walls may not be real">
  A huge offer at one price – a "wall" – looks like solid support. But someone
  can pull it within a second. Placing fake offers nobody intends to fill is
  banned manipulation on regulated markets, and it still happens.
</Callout>

## Common myths

- **"I will buy at the last price."** You buy at the lowest sell offer – and with a bigger amount, higher.
- **"A big wall will stop the price."** As long as it is there. Walls can vanish.
- **"The spread is the exchange's fee."** The spread is the gap between buyers and sellers; the fee is paid
  on top.

<Callout title="Summary">

- The order book: red sells at the top, green buys at the bottom; the gap between the best is the spread.
- A market buy takes offers step by step – the bigger it is, the higher the average price (slippage).
- The depth chart shows the book as two mountains; steep mountains = many offers near the price.
- Walls in the book can disappear – do not rely on them.

</Callout>
