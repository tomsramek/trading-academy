## Four tools are enough

- **Horizontal line** – a level where the price used to stop:
  [support or resistance](/courses/trading-with-a-plan/support-and-resistance-in-depth). The most used tool
  of all.
- **Trend line** – connects two lows (or highs) and shows the slope of the trend.
- **Rectangle** – marks a price zone. Support and resistance are more often bands than exact lines.
- **Measure tool** – drag from one point to another and the chart shows the price change in percent and
  the number of candles.

Handy extras: the **magnet** snaps a line exactly to candle prices, and the **bin** deletes all drawings
at once.

## The price alert

An alert (the bell by the chart or in the menu) sends you a message when the price crosses a chosen level:

1. Choose the pair and the price – for example a level you have drawn.
2. Choose the direction: **crossing up**, **crossing down** or **either**.
3. Choose where the message goes: the app, email or both.

An alert costs you nothing and buys nothing – it just lets you know. Instead of looking at the chart ten
times a day, set two levels and go live your life.

<Callout type="warning" title="Drawings can get lost">
  On some exchanges drawings are saved only in the browser or only for one chart
  view. After switching devices or chart types you may not find them. So note
  important levels in your journal too.
</Callout>

## Common myths

- **"The more lines, the better the analysis."** A chart crowded with lines only shows what you want to see.
- **"The alert will buy for me."** An alert only informs. Buying or selling is a separate order.
- **"A level must fit to the exact dollar."** Draw zones instead – the price rarely returns to a level
  exactly.
