The indicator menu is like a buffet: twenty dishes and everything looks tempting. Whoever takes some of
everything ends up with so many lines on the chart that the candles disappear under them. Professor Wick
calls it indicator soup – and does not recommend it.

<Illustration name="indicatorSoup" />

## Two kinds of indicators

- **Over the candles** (main chart) – lines in the same units as the price: averages, bands.
- **Below the chart** (panel) – their own scale: volume, oscillators, momentum.

## Where the menu is on Binance

On Binance you open the indicators with an icon above the chart. The dialog has two tabs: **Main Indicator**
(over the candles) and **Sub Indicator** (below the chart). For each indicator you set the length, colour
and line width.

## Over the candles

| Abbreviation | What it is in one sentence                                                                                                                 |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **MA**       | <KeyTerm term="moving-average">moving average</KeyTerm> – the average price over the last N candles; smooths out noise and shows direction |
| **EMA**      | exponential average – like MA, but recent candles weigh more, so it reacts faster                                                          |
| **BOLL**     | <KeyTerm term="bollinger-bands">Bollinger Bands</KeyTerm> – an average with two bands around it; the wilder the market, the wider they get |
| **SAR**      | dots above or below the candles that "trail" the price; when they flip to the other side, the trend may be turning                         |
| **VWAP**     | volume-weighted average price – what those who traded paid on average                                                                      |
| **WMA**      | weighted average – recent candles weigh more, similar to EMA                                                                               |
| **AVL**      | the day's average price – a line of the average trade price                                                                                |
| **TRIX**     | a triple-smoothed average showing the pace of the trend                                                                                    |
| **SUPER**    | Supertrend – a line below the price in a rise and above it in a fall, derived from ATR                                                     |

## Below the chart

| Abbreviation | What it is in one sentence                                                                                                           |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **VOL**      | <KeyTerm term="volume">volume</KeyTerm> – how much was traded in each candle                                                         |
| **MACD**     | <KeyTerm term="macd">MACD</KeyTerm> – the difference of two averages and its signal line; shows the strength and turning of a trend  |
| **RSI**      | <KeyTerm term="rsi">RSI</KeyTerm> – the strength of rises against falls on a 0–100 scale; above 70 "overbought", below 30 "oversold" |
| **StochRSI** | the RSI of the RSI – even more sensitive and even more jumpy                                                                         |
| **KDJ**      | a relative of the stochastic oscillator – where the price is within the range of the last candles                                    |
| **OBV**      | volume added on up candles and subtracted on down candles                                                                            |
| **ATR**      | the average range of a candle – measures volatility (not in Binance's menu; Supertrend is built from it)                             |
| **MFI**      | like RSI, but also takes volume into account                                                                                         |
| **CCI**      | how far the price is from its average                                                                                                |
| **WR**       | Williams %R – where the close sits within the range of the last candles                                                              |

## Three to start with

On many exchanges the chart opens with a trio of averages, **MA(7), MA(25) and MA(99)** – the number in
brackets is the number of candles. Here is how it looks with volume and RSI on Bitcoin's daily chart:

To start, this is enough:

1. **VOL** – shows whether a move has interest behind it.
2. **One or two averages** – the default MAs, or longer ones (50 and 200 days) for the direction of the
   trend.
3. **RSI** – whether the market is running too fast in one direction.

Turn the others on once you know what you need them for. How to use indicators in depth is taught in the
[Trading with a plan](/courses/trading-with-a-plan) course.

<Callout type="warning" title="An indicator does not see the future">
  All indicators are calculated from past prices. None knows where the price
  will go tomorrow. More indicators do not give more certainty – just more lines
  that can contradict each other.
</Callout>

## Common myths

- **"The more indicators, the more accurate the signal."** Most measure the same thing with a different
  equation – and contradict each other.
- **"RSI above 70 = sell."** A strong trend can stay "overbought" for weeks.
- **"The default settings are the best."** They are just the defaults, not a recommendation.

<Callout title="Summary">

- Over the candles: MA, EMA, BOLL, SAR, VWAP. Below the chart: VOL, MACD, RSI, StochRSI, KDJ, OBV, ATR.
- The number in brackets (MA(7)) is the number of candles the indicator is calculated from.
- Volume, one or two averages and RSI are enough to start.
- Indicators are calculated from the past; more lines do not mean more certainty.

</Callout>
