<Callout title="In this lesson">

- learn to state a trading idea as a hypothesis
- turn it into exact rules with no room for "by eye"
- find out why fewer rules are often more

</Callout>

"I buy when it looks strong and sell when it stops." That is how many people trade – and they never
find out whether it works, because "looks strong" means something different every day. You can only
test what is written so precisely that a computer could follow it.

<Illustration name="recipeChef" />

## 1. Hypothesis: why it should work

Every strategy starts with the sentence "the market tends to…". For example: **"When Bitcoin is in a
long-term uptrend, it tends to continue in it."** And right after: **why**? Because people react to news
slowly, trends attract more buyers, big players buy gradually.

A hypothesis without an explanation is dangerous. If you find a pattern that makes no sense ("it rises
on Tuesdays after a full moon"), you most likely found chance that will not repeat.

## 2. Rules: exact and complete

Now you turn the hypothesis into <KeyTerm term="trading-rules">trading rules</KeyTerm>. They must
answer everything that is decided in a trade:

| Question                | Example of an exact rule                     |
| ----------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| What do I trade?        | BTC/USDT, spot                               |
| On which timeframe?     | daily candles, decision after the day closes |
| When do I enter?        | closing price above SMA 200                  |
| When do I exit?         | closing price below SMA 200                  |
| Where is the stop-loss? | 2 ATR below the entry                        |
| How big a position?     | 1% of the account at risk                    |
| What does a trade cost? | a 0.1% fee on both buying and selling        |

The test of rules: give them to two people and let them go through the same chart. If they make
different trades, the rules are not precise enough.

## 3. Less is more

Every extra rule and every number that can be tuned (an average length, an RSI threshold, a stop-loss
percentage) gives the strategy more chances to "learn the past by heart". In the previous course we
called that overfitting.

- Start with **as few rules as possible** that still make sense.
- Prefer **round, common numbers** (20, 50, 200) to "discoveries" like 137.
- Every extra rule needs a **reason**, not just a better test result.

<Callout type="warning" title="Rules are not a promise">
  Exact rules do not guarantee a profit. They only guarantee that you can find
  out honestly whether the strategy worked in the past – and that you know
  exactly what you are doing when you trade.
</Callout>

## Common myths

- **"I have the rules in my head."** Then they cannot be tested and they change with your mood.
- **"I will add a filter and the results will improve."** In a test almost always. In reality often not.
- **"Why it works does not matter, as long as it works."** Without a reason you will not notice when it
  stops working.

<Callout title="Summary">

- A strategy starts with a hypothesis: what the market does and why.
- The rules must say exactly what, when, how much and at what cost – so that two people apply them the
  same way.
- Fewer rules and common numbers mean less risk of overfitting.
- Exact rules are a condition for testing, not a guarantee of profit.

</Callout>
