- understand how their win rates and profit sizes differ

There are thousands of strategies, but most belong to a few families. Each family has its "character":
how often it wins, how big its profits and losses are and in which market regime it thrives. When you
know the character, its usual behaviour will not scare you.

<Illustration name="strategyToolbox" />

## Trend following

**Idea:** what rises will keep rising – until the trend ends.
**Example:** hold as long as the price is above a long moving average.

A <KeyTerm term="trend-following">trend-following</KeyTerm> strategy has a strange
character: **most trades end with a small loss**, because a trend often fails to
take off. The money is made by a few big trends that pay for all those small
losses. In the example test from module 3 only 31% of trades were profitable –
and yet the result was positive thanks to a few huge rallies.

- **Thrives:** in strong trends.
- **Suffers:** in a range, where it gives one false signal after another.
- **Psychologically:** hard – long streaks of small losses and waiting for the big trend.

## Mean reversion

**Idea:** a price that has moved too far from its average will return to it.
**Example:** buy when RSI falls below 30 in a range, sell around the middle of the range.

A <KeyTerm term="mean-reversion">mean-reversion</KeyTerm> strategy is the mirror of trend
following: **it wins often, but little**. Then comes a market that does not
return – a fall that keeps going – and one big loss wipes out months of small
profits.

- **Thrives:** in a calm range.
- **Suffers:** in a strong trend and in a crash ("cheap" gets cheaper).
- **Psychologically:** pleasant – until that one big loss arrives. Without a stop-loss it is outright
  dangerous.

## Breakouts

**Idea:** when the price breaks out of a range or a long-term high, it often keeps going.
**Example:** buy when the day closes above the highest high of the last 20 days.

Breakouts are related to trend following: they catch the start of a move, but **many breakouts are
false**. The win rate tends to be low, and the result comes from the breakouts that really take off – like
November 2024 above 73,800.

## Character in numbers

| Type            | Win rate | Profits vs losses                     | Worst regime               |
| --------------- | -------- | ------------------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Trend following | low      | big profits, small losses             | range                      |
| Mean reversion  | high     | small profits, an occasional big loss | strong trend, crash        |
| Breakouts       | low      | big profits, many small losses        | range with false breakouts |

<Callout type="warning" title="Every strategy has its bad period">
  No type of strategy works in all regimes. Anyone expecting a strategy without
  long losing periods is expecting something that does not exist – and will then
  abandon a good strategy right at its bottom.
</Callout>

## Common myths

- **"I want a strategy that wins 80% of trades."** Such strategies often lose everything at once every
  now and then.
- **"Five losses in a row, the strategy does not work."** For trend following that is a normal day at the
  office.
- **"I will combine all three types and it will always work."** Combinations have a character too.

<Callout title="Summary">

- Trend following: low win rate, big profits, suffers in a range.
- Mean reversion: high win rate, small profits, an occasional big loss, suffers in trends and crashes.
- Breakouts: catch the start of a move, but many are false.
- Knowing a strategy's character means not giving up on it in its normal bad period.

</Callout>
