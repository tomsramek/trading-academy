In the last lesson the 40-day average strategy looked great. But we chose that length precisely from that
data – so the test really only showed that we can find the best number in the past. The real question
is: **how would it have done on data it has never seen?**

## Two piles of data

The data is split in advance, before the very first test:

- **in-sample** – where you invent and tune the strategy (for us 2019–2021),
- **<KeyTerm term="out-of-sample">out-of-sample</KeyTerm>** – you must not touch it until the rules are
  finished (for us January 2022 to September 2025).

The out-of-sample test is done **once**. Anyone who looks at the result, adjusts the rules and tests again
has turned "out-of-sample" into more tuning data.

What happened:

- The "best" 40-day average **slightly beat simply holding** on the new data and had smaller drawdowns –
  but among the 30 tested lengths it came only **20th**. Its brilliance in 2019–2021 was largely chance.
- The 200-day average did better. Does that prove it is better? **No.** It has only 13 trades, and we have
  just chosen it – based on the out-of-sample data. If we started using it, we would overfit again, just
  on different years.

## Walk-forward

The <KeyTerm term="walk-forward">walk-forward</KeyTerm> method repeats the split step by
step, the way a real trader would:

1. Pick the best average length on the last three years.
2. Trade with it for the following year.
3. Move forward a year and repeat.

| Year          | Chosen length | Strategy | Buy and hold |
| ------------- | ------------- | -------- | ------------ |
| 2022          | 40            | −44%     | −64%         |
| 2023          | 40            | +136%    | +156%        |
| 2024          | 110           | +93%     | +121%        |
| 2025 (to Sep) | 170           | −1%      | +22%         |

In total **+153%** against **+147%** for simply holding. The strategy lost less in the 2022 crash and made
less in the rallies. No miracle – and that is valuable information.

<Callout type="warning" title="An honest result tends to be boring">
  When a strategy passes an out-of-sample and walk-forward test and comes out
  "roughly like simply holding, with smaller drawdowns", that is a realistic
  result. Miraculous numbers usually mean a mistake in the test.
</Callout>

## Common myths

- **"It did not work out of sample, so I will tune it."** That turns it into tuning data.
- **"The 200-day average did best, I will use it."** You chose it by its result – the same trap again.
- **"Walk-forward only matched holding, the strategy is useless."** Smaller drawdowns can be worth
  something.

<Callout title="Summary">

- Split the data in advance: for tuning (in-sample) and for a single final test (out-of-sample).
- Our winner of 2019–2021 ended up in the middle of the field on new data.
- Walk-forward simulates real trading: it picks parameters only from the past.
- A realistic result tends to be modest; miracles usually mean a mistake.

</Callout>
