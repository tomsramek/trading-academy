A <KeyTerm term="backtest">backtest</KeyTerm> means running a strategy's exact rules over historical data
and seeing how it would have done. It is the cheapest way to throw away a bad idea – and also the
cheapest way to fool yourself. In this module we test one simple strategy on real Bitcoin data.

<Callout type="risk" title="An example of the method, not a recommendation">
  The strategy in this module only explains how testing works and where mistakes
  happen. It is not a trading recommendation, and past results do not guarantee
  future results.
</Callout>

## The strategy we test

**Hold Bitcoin while its daily closing price is above a moving average, otherwise hold cash.** The
decision is made after the day closes, with a 0.1% fee on every purchase and sale. We test on daily
BTC/USDT data from Binance.

<Illustration name="timeTraveler" />

## Trap 1: look-ahead

The most treacherous mistake is when the test uses information you would not yet have at the time. For
example: "if the price closed above the average today, hold Bitcoin **today**". But you only know today's
close at the end of the day – when today's move is already behind you.

The same strategy with a 40-day average in 2019–2021:

- **correctly** (the decision applies from the next day): **+1,747%**,
- **with look-ahead**: **+24,015%** and **100% winning trades**.

Whenever a backtest looks too good to be true – 100% winning trades, no drawdown – it almost certainly
contains look-ahead.

## Trap 2: survivors

When you test a strategy on the cryptocurrencies that are in the top ten today, you only test on the
winners. Coins that collapsed in the meantime – like LUNA in 2022 – are missing from the test, so the
result is better than it would have been in reality. This is called <KeyTerm term="survivorship-bias">survivorship bias</KeyTerm>.

## Trap 3: overfitting

We tried average lengths from 10 to 300 days. The best in 2019–2021 was a **40-day** average – better than
the 29 other options. How it did on the years it had not seen we will see in lesson 10. Spoiler: not as well.

## Trap 4: costs and slippage

Without fees the strategy would have made 1,857% instead of 1,747% in 2019–2021. Here the difference is
small because the strategy traded only 29 times in three years. A strategy with hundreds of trades a
year, however, can be profitable only on paper – you know what a trade costs from the previous course.

## Trap 5: data that is not reality

The test assumes you always buy exactly at the closing price. In reality there is the spread, slippage
and exchange outages. For big coins on a daily chart it is a detail; for small coins and short
timeframes it is crucial.

## Common myths

- **"The backtest made thousands of percent, I am going for it."** Look for the mistake first. There
  usually is one.
- **"I tested on the top 10 coins."** That is, on the ones that survived.
- **"I will ignore fees, they are small."** With frequent trading they are the main result.

<Callout title="Summary">

- A backtest shows how the rules would have done in the past – nothing more.
- Look-ahead turns a strategy into a miracle; a 100% win rate is a warning sign.
- Survivorship bias, overfitting and missing costs inflate the result.
- The test assumes ideal trades; reality is more expensive.

</Callout>
