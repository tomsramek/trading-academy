In the previous course we said that leveraged futures are not for beginners. That still holds. But the
leveraged market is huge and its data is public – and it reveals how overheated the market is, even if
you only trade on spot.

## Funding as a thermometer

A reminder: <KeyTerm term="funding-rate">funding</KeyTerm> is paid between perpetual futures traders
to keep the contract price close to spot. When the contract trades above spot (more demand for longs),
longs pay shorts.

What interests us is **how high funding is**. Annualised, it shows how much people are willing to pay
to bet on a rise with leverage:

- The median daily funding for Bitcoin since 2021 comes to roughly **9% a year**.
- In February 2021 the weekly average was over **120% a year** – the market was full of leverage.
- In March 2024, around the high at the time, around **50% a year**.
- After the collapse of the FTX exchange in November 2022, funding was **negative** – more people were
  betting on a fall.

## Open interest

The indicator <KeyTerm term="open-interest">open interest</KeyTerm> is the number of open
contracts – how much leveraged money is in the market right now. It is read
together with the price:

- **price rises and open interest rises** – new leverage is flowing in; the rally has fuel, and risk,
- **price rises and open interest falls** – shorts are closing positions, often a short-lived spike,
- **price falls and open interest drops sharply** – positions are being closed or liquidated.

## A liquidation cascade

When there is a lot of leverage in the market, a smaller move is enough to start liquidations. The
exchange sells liquidated longs at market – which pushes the price down, which triggers more
liquidations. A cascade forms that throws the price far beyond a "reasonable" level for a few hours.
That is where the long wicks on daily candles come from – like on 5 March 2024 or 5 August 2024.

## How to use it

- **Extremely high funding = caution.** It does not say the price will fall tomorrow. It says the
  market is crowded and a fall could be sharp.
- **Negative funding in a crash = fear.** A market full of shorts can shoot up quickly once the shorts
  start covering.
- **Derivatives data is context**, just like macro. It is part of your picture of the market, not a
  trading signal.

<Callout
  type="warning"
  title="An overheated market can stay overheated for a long time"
>
  High funding can last for weeks while the price keeps rising. Anyone betting
  against the market because of it pays just like someone who sells only because
  RSI is above 70.
</Callout>

## Common myths

- **"Funding is high, so I am shorting."** A crowded market can rise for longer than you can hold out.
- **"Open interest is rising, so the price will rise."** Only the amount of leverage is rising – on
  both sides.
- **"I do not trade futures, so it does not concern me."** Liquidations on futures move the spot price
  too.

- Annualised funding shows how much people pay for leverage on a rise; extremes signal overheating or

- Open interest = the amount of leverage in the market; read it together with the price.
- Liquidation cascades turn smaller moves into sharp drops and long wicks.
- Derivatives data is context, not a trading signal.
