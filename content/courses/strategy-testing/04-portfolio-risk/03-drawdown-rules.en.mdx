You know from the previous course that a drawdown is harder to recover than to fall into, and you have
daily and weekly loss limits. They protect you from one bad day. This lesson is about a worse enemy: a slow
drawdown over weeks and months, when every single trade looks fine – and the account still keeps falling.

<Illustration name="drawdownStairs" />

## What drawdown is normal

Every strategy has drawdowns. The question is not "whether" but "how big is still normal". A backtest
gives you the answer, or a simulation like the one in the previous lesson.

Example: a strategy with a 40% win rate, a 2R win and a **2%** risk per trade. In a simulation of 200
trades, the median largest drawdown is **23%**, and only 5% of runs fall by more than **38%**. The longest
losing streak tends to be around **9 trades**.

For this strategy, a 20% drawdown is unpleasant but normal. A 45% drawdown is no longer normal – something
has most likely changed. Without this knowledge you would panic at 20%, or still be waiting at 45%.

## Tiered rules

The rules are set **in advance** and measured from the last peak of the <KeyTerm term="equity-curve">equity curve</KeyTerm>:

| Drawdown from peak    | What you do                                                                          |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 10%                   | Halve the risk per trade. Go through your journal: are you following rules?          |
| 20%                   | Risk down to a quarter. Compare results with the backtest – win rate, wins, streaks. |
| Beyond the test limit | Stop. The strategy goes back to a forward test without money.                        |
| New peak              | Full risk back.                                                                      |

The "beyond the test limit" row is the switch-off rule from the forward-testing lesson: for example 1.5
times the largest drawdown in the backtest.

## What the rule costs and what it gains

A simulation with the same strategy (2% risk, 200 trades, 20,000 runs; only the 10% and 20% steps). Once the strategy works (40% win
rate), once it has stopped working (33% win rate – the expected value is almost zero):

| Win rate            | Rules  | Account (median) | Drawdown exceeded by only 5% of runs | Chance of a 30%+ drawdown |
| ------------------- | ------ | ---------------- | ------------------------------------ | ------------------------- |
| 40% (works)         | none   | 2.04×            | 38%                                  | 19%                       |
| 40% (works)         | tiered | 1.71×            | 24%                                  | almost none               |
| 33% (does not work) | none   | 0.89×            | 61%                                  | 75%                       |
| 33% (does not work) | tiered | 0.90×            | 32%                                  | 10%                       |

The rule is **insurance**: when the strategy works, it costs you part of the profit (2.04× against 1.71×),
because after a drawdown you recover with smaller positions. When the strategy stops working, it prevents
the worst – in the worst runs it turns a 61% drawdown into 32%. And as long as you do not know which of the
two situations you are in, only a rule can decide.

<Callout type="risk" title="A simulation, not a forecast">
  The numbers are a random simulation of a made-up strategy. They show how the
  rules work, not how much you will make. A real market also has slippage, price
  gaps and crashes the simulation does not know about.
</Callout>

## Why not "just a bit longer"

In a drawdown the brain switches: it wants to win the loss back fast (bigger positions) or not see it (stop
looking and change nothing). Both are the opposite of what the rule does. That is why you:

- **write the rules in advance** into your trading plan, with numbers,
- **calculate from the account, not from a feeling** – once a week, note where the account is against its
  peak,
- **do not change them during a drawdown.** Make changes only after a review, calmly, and test them.

## Common myths

- **"After a drawdown I have to risk more to catch up."** That is exactly how accounts blow up.
- **"Cutting risk = admitting the strategy does not work."** No – it is insurance for when you do not know.
- **"A 20% drawdown? The strategy is broken."** It depends on what is normal for it. Find out in advance.

<Callout title="Summary">

- Find out from a backtest or simulation what drawdown is normal for your strategy.
- Step down risk by rules set in advance: say half at 10%, a quarter at 20%, stop beyond the test limit.
- The rule costs part of the profit when everything works and saves the account when it does not.
- Do not change the rules during a drawdown.

</Callout>
