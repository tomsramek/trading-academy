- learn a regular review that takes an hour a month

- separate execution mistakes from the swings of the strategy

The journal from the previous course is raw material. On its own it improves nothing – a regular review
does. Professional traders do it like closing the books: on a fixed day, using the same template, no matter
whether the month was good or bad.

<Illustration name="reportCard" />

## The numbers: compare yourself with the backtest

Once a month, calculate the same metrics as in the testing module and put them side by side:

| Sample month         | Backtest    | Reality       |
| -------------------- | ----------- | ------------- |
| Number of trades     | 8 a month   | 11            |
| Win rate             | 40%         | 36%           |
| Average win / loss   | +2.0R / −1R | +1.8R / −1.2R |
| Result for the month | –           | −1.2R         |

_Sample numbers, not real trades._

What the table tells you:

- **More trades than there should be.** The strategy gives about 8 signals a month. Where did the three
  extra come from?
- **Losses are bigger than 1R.** An average loss of −1.2R means the stop-losses are slipping – through
  slippage, or because you move them.
- **A 36% win rate against 40%** means nothing over 11 trades. On such a small sample it is noise.

Pay more attention to results in <KeyTerm term="r-multiple">R</KeyTerm> than in money. Money changes with
the size of the account; R shows how well you trade.

## Execution mistakes

Mark every trade in the journal with one question: **did I follow the rules?** Then split the results:

- **trades by the rules** – they judge the strategy,
- **trades outside the rules** – they judge you.

If the trades outside the rules lose money (and they usually do), you do not have a problem with the
strategy. You have a problem with discipline, and that is solved differently – limits, less time at the
chart, clearer rules.

## A decision is not a result

A good decision can end in a loss and a bad one in a profit. That is why every trade gets two grades:

|                       | Profit            | Loss                       |
| --------------------- | ----------------- | -------------------------- |
| **By the rules**      | a deserved reward | the normal cost of trading |
| **Outside the rules** | dangerous luck    | a deserved lesson          |

The most dangerous box is the bottom left: you broke the rules and made money. The brain remembers that it
works – and next time tries it with a bigger position.

## A monthly review template

1. **Numbers:** number of trades, win rate, average win and loss in R, result, drawdown from the peak.
2. **Comparison with the backtest:** are the numbers within the expected range?
3. **Discipline:** how many trades were outside the rules and how did they end?
4. **Costs:** fees and slippage against what the backtest assumed.
5. **One change:** at most one adjustment for next month – in behaviour, not in the strategy.

<Callout type="warning" title="Do not change the strategy every month">
  A month is too short to judge a strategy – it is 5 to 20 trades. The monthly
  review mainly watches you. You decide about the strategy only after dozens of
  trades – that is what the next lesson is about.
</Callout>

## Common myths

- **"The month was profitable, I do not need a review."** A profitable month with broken rules is a
  warning, not a success.
- **"I judge in money, that is what I want."** Money mixes up the size of the account with the quality of
  trading.
- **"Bad month = bad strategy."** First check whether you traded it at all.

<Callout title="Summary">

- Review regularly, with the same template, whatever the month was like.
- Compare real numbers in R with the backtest – number of trades, win rate, wins, losses, costs.
- Separate trades by the rules (they judge the strategy) from trades outside them (they judge you).
- A good decision is not the same as a good result.

</Callout>
