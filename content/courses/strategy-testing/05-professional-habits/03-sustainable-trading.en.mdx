Congratulations – you have finished the academy's hardest course. You can read the market context, build
a strategy from rules, test it so that it does not fool you and manage the risk of the whole account. That
is more than most people who trade ever do.

<Illustration name="hammockTrader" />

## What to take away from the course

- **Context:** the market switches between regimes, cryptocurrencies move together and volatility changes
  – measure it with ATR.
- **Strategy:** idea → hypothesis → precise rules. Stop-loss and position size based on volatility.
- **Testing:** a backtest has traps; what decides is out-of-sample testing, walk-forward and a forward
  test. An honest result tends to be boring.
- **Risk:** correlated positions are one bet, risk only a fraction of the Kelly number and have drawdown
  rules.
- **Habits:** a regular review, decisions by the number of trades, not by feeling.

## Does it pay off?

An honest question few people ask. Example: an account of 4,000 €, a very decent annual profit of 10%, so
400 €. The strategy takes 5 hours a week – watching, trades, journal, review. That is about 260 hours a
year, so roughly **1.50 € an hour**.

And you know from the testing module that even a good strategy did roughly as well as simply holding out
of sample – just with smaller drawdowns. Trading makes sense when you enjoy it, when you want to learn and
when you do it with money you can afford to lose. As a quick road to wealth, it does not work.

## Habits for years

- **The crypto market never sleeps, you do.** Set price alerts instead of watching the chart. Overnight
  and on holiday, no open positions without a stop-loss.
- **A fixed time for trading.** Say an hour in the evening and a review once a month. Outside that time,
  the exchange app stays closed.
- **Breaks are part of the plan.** After hitting a loss limit, when ill, under stress or after a big win.
- **Records for taxes.** Crypto gains are usually taxed, and from 2026 crypto service providers in the EU
  collect data on their clients' transactions for tax authorities under the <KeyTerm term="dac8">DAC8</KeyTerm> directive (first reports in 2027). Keep track of every trade and check the rules with a tax advisor.

## What to learn next

- **Programming backtests.** A spreadsheet is enough to start; a Python script tests hundreds of variants
  and does not forget fees. Just remember that more variants mean more chances to overfit.
- **Statistics.** Probability, variance, sample size – the most useful "indicators" there are.
- **Your own data.** Many exchanges offer public historical data for free – the charts in this course are
  built on it too.
- **Healthy scepticism.** Towards every strategy that looks too good – including your own.
