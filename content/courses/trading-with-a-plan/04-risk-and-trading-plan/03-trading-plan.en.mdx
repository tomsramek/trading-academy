Pilots use a checklist even after ten thousand hours of flying. Not because they cannot fly, but
because everyone makes mistakes under pressure. A <KeyTerm term="trading-plan">trading plan</KeyTerm>
is the same thing for trading: you make all the important decisions in advance, with a clear head –
and in the trade you only follow them.

## What belongs in the plan

1. **Markets and timeframes** – what you trade (say only BTC and ETH) and on which charts (week, day,
   four hours).
2. **The situation you are looking for** – described exactly. Not "when it looks good", but for
   example "price above a rising SMA 200, a retest of a support zone on the daily chart".
3. **Entry** – what has to happen for you to buy, and with which order.
4. **Stop-loss** – where exactly and why there (where your idea turns out wrong).
5. **Exit** – a profit target, a trailing stop or another rule. And when you end the trade even if it
   reached neither the target nor the stop-loss.
6. **Position size** – risk per trade (say 1%) and the formula.
7. **Limits** – how much you may lose in a day, a week and a month before you stop trading.
8. **When you do not trade** – before major news, when you are tired, angry or after a losing streak.
9. **Review** – when and how you go through your journal (more in the next lesson).

## An example of a one-page plan

| Item           | My rule                                                                |
| -------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Markets        | BTC/USDT, spot, no leverage                                            |
| Timeframes     | week for direction, day for structure and zones, 4 hours for entry     |
| Situation      | price above a rising SMA 200 (day) and a retest of a broken resistance |
| Entry          | limit order in the zone, only after a 4h candle closes above the zone  |
| Stop-loss      | below the bottom of the zone, at least 1.5% below the entry            |
| Exit           | half at +2R, the rest with an 8% trailing stop                         |
| Risk           | 1% of the account per trade, at most 2 open trades                     |
| Limits         | −3% in a week or 4 losses in a row = a week off                        |
| I do not trade | on major news days, after midnight, when I am angry                    |
| Review         | journal every Sunday, statistics in R every month                      |

This is not a recommended strategy – it is an example of what a specific plan looks like. Yours can be
completely different. What matters is that it is **written down and unambiguous**: if someone else got
it, they would make the same trades.

## Limits: the emergency brake

The most important part of the plan is the one most often forgotten: **when to stop**. After a losing
streak people try to "win it back" – they increase positions and break rules. A firm limit (say a week
off after −3%) stops the damage before a bad week turns into a bad year.

<Callout type="warning" title="A plan you do not follow is not a plan">
  Most traders have some kind of plan – in their head. But a plan in your head
  is easy to change in the middle of a trade. Write it down and change it only
  at the weekend, never with an open position.
</Callout>

## Common myths

- **"I have the plan in my head."** A plan in your head changes with your mood.
- **"The plan restricts me."** That is the point – it restricts the decisions you make under stress.
- **"If I break the plan and it works out, it does not matter."** It does. You learned that breaking
  rules pays off.

<Callout title="Summary">

- A trading plan is a list of decisions made in advance, with a clear head.
- It covers markets, the situation, entry, stop-loss, exit, position size, limits and review.
- It has to be written down and so unambiguous that someone else would make the same trades from it.
- Loss limits stop you before a bad week turns into a bad year.

</Callout>
