"My strategy has a 70% win rate!" Sounds great. But without a second number it means nothing at all.
This lesson is about telling whether a way of trading has any chance of working.

## Two numbers, not one

Every strategy is described by two numbers:

- **the win rate** – what percentage of trades end in profit,
- **the average win and the average loss** – in the R-multiples from the last lesson.

Only together do they give the <KeyTerm term="expected-value">expected value</KeyTerm> – how many R
you make or lose on one trade on average:

**Expected value = win rate × average win − loss rate × average loss**

## Two examples

**Strategy A:** a 40% win rate, wins of +2R, losses of −1R.
0.4 × 2 − 0.6 × 1 = **+0.2R** per trade. Most trades end in a loss, and yet the strategy is positive on
average.

**Strategy B:** a 70% win rate, wins of +0.4R, losses of −1R.
0.7 × 0.4 − 0.3 × 1 = **−0.02R** per trade. Seven trades out of ten work out – and you still lose in the
long run.

And that is before costs. If fees and slippage cost, say, 0.1R per trade, strategy A makes +0.1R and strategy B ends at −0.12R.

<Callout type="warning" title="A positive expected value is rare">
  The numbers above are an example of the calculation, not a promise. Most
  people who trade do not have a positive expected value after costs – that is
  why most of them lose. Finding out whether you have one takes a long time and
  costs money.
</Callout>

## Losing streaks are normal

Even a strategy with a positive expected value has long losing streaks. With a 40% win rate over 100
trades:

- **5 losses in a row** happen almost certainly (in 98% of cases in a simulation),
- **8 losses in a row** in roughly every second case,
- **10 losses in a row** in roughly every fifth.

That is why you risk 1% and not 10%. And that is why a streak of five losses on its own does not mean
the strategy does not work.

## How many trades you need

After ten trades you know nothing – luck decided. After fifty you have a first idea. A more reliable
picture comes from **a hundred or more trades** under the same rules. Anyone who changes strategy after
every three losses will never find out whether any of them worked.

## Common myths

- **"I win 7 out of 10, I am good."** It depends on how much you win and how much you lose.
- **"Five losses in a row – the strategy is broken."** With a 40% win rate that is common.
- **"I am up for the month, it works."** A month is too short to prove anything – it may have been luck.

<Callout title="Summary">

- The win rate without the size of wins and losses says nothing.
- Expected value = win rate × average win − loss rate × average loss (in R).
- Costs reduce the expected value of every trade.
- Long losing streaks are normal; you only know something about a strategy after a hundred trades.

</Callout>
