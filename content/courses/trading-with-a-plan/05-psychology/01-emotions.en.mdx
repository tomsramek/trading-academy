Your plan is written, your risk is calculated. And then the price jumps 8% in ten minutes and suddenly
you cannot see the plan. Emotions are not a bug you can switch off – they are old mechanisms that once
helped us survive. In the market, though, they usually do harm.

## A loss hurts twice as much

Psychologists Daniel Kahneman and Amos Tversky found that a loss hurts us roughly **twice as much** as
an equal gain pleases us. This is called <KeyTerm term="loss-aversion">loss aversion</KeyTerm>, and in
trading it has a nasty consequence:

- **we take profits too early** – "so I do not lose them",
- **we hold losses too long** – "it will come back, then I will sell".

Researchers call this the disposition effect and have documented it in thousands of real investors.
The result: small profits, big losses. Exactly the opposite of what you need.

## FOMO

The abbreviation <KeyTerm term="fomo">FOMO</KeyTerm> (fear of missing out) stands for the fear that something will slip
away. The price rises, everyone on social media celebrates and you buy – without a plan, without a
stop-loss, often right at the top. The more the price has risen, the stronger the FOMO tends to be.
And the worse the entry.

## Fear

Fear does the opposite: after a few losses you will not enter even a trade that fits your plan
exactly. Or you close a good trade at the first small wave against you. Fear is often a reaction to
too much risk – when one loss really hurts, you are risking too much.

## Revenge trading

After a loss comes the urge to "win it back". Right now, with a bigger position, without a plan. This
is called <KeyTerm term="revenge-trading">revenge trading</KeyTerm>. It is the fastest way to turn one
loss into five.

<Callout type="warning" title="The market does not know you lost">
  The market owes you nothing, and "winning it back" is not a reason to trade.
  After a loss the best trade is no trade – at least until tomorrow.
</Callout>

## Safeguards against emotions

- **Decide in advance.** Enter the entry, stop-loss and target as orders right when you open the
  trade. Then there is nothing left to decide under pressure.
- **Risk so little that a loss barely matters.** 1% of the account should not ruin your evening.
- **The 24-hour rule.** When something tempts you outside the plan, wait a day. Many "unique opportunities" are gone by tomorrow.
- **Limits in the plan.** Once you hit the daily or weekly loss limit, you stop. No debate.
- **Write your feelings in the journal.** In a few weeks you will see which emotions cost you the most.

## Common myths

- **"I have no emotions, I trade rationally."** Everyone has them. The difference is whether you plan
  for them.
- **"This time is different, I have to buy now."** That is exactly what FOMO says.
- **"Just one trade to get back to zero."** That is how revenge trading starts.

<Callout title="Summary">

- A loss hurts roughly twice as much as a gain pleases – so we take profits early and hold losses long.
- FOMO pushes you to buy at the top, fear blocks even good trades.
- Revenge trading turns one loss into several.
- Decide in advance, risk little, set limits and give yourself time to think.

</Callout>
