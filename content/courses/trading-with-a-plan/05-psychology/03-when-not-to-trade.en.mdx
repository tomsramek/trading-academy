The whole course was about trading deliberately. The last lesson is about the skill that is hardest to
learn: **not trading**. No position is also a position – and often the best one.

<Illustration name="hammockRest" />

## When it is better not to trade

- **When you have no plan** or the situation does not fit it. "Almost fits" means "does not fit".
- **After you hit your loss limit.** The plan says stop – so stop.
- **When you are tired, ill, angry or euphoric.** Decisions in a bad state are expensive.
- **Before and during major news** – central bank decisions, for example. The price can swing both
  ways and the spread widens.
- **When the market has no clear structure.** In a messy range, indicators and structure give one false
  signal after another.
- **With money you will need.** Rent, savings, loan payments – they never belong in the market.
- **When it has stopped being interesting and you trade out of boredom.** Boredom is an expensive
  reason.

## What you took away from the course

- **Market structure** – highs and lows show the direction and tell you where you are wrong.
- **Support and resistance zones** – not exact lines; breakout, retest and role reversal.
- **Indicators** – moving averages, RSI and volume as aids, not prophecy; they come late.
- **Orders and costs** – stop-market, OCO, trailing stop; fees and slippage that add up.
- **Risk** – position size from risk, results in R, expected value and normal losing streaks.
- **Plan and journal** – decisions made in advance, loss limits, review by the numbers.
- **Psychology** – emotions and thinking traps, which rules help against, not willpower.

## How to try out your plan safely

1. **Write the plan** on one page following the lesson on the trading plan.
2. **Try it without money.** On a demo account or just in a paper journal, make at least 50, ideally 100 trades by the rules. Work out the result in R, including costs – the first 30 to 50 trades are only a first check, not proof.
3. **If the numbers make sense, move to small amounts.** So small that a loss does not bother you. Real
   money brings emotions that paper does not.
4. **Review every week** and change the plan only at the weekend.
5. **Accept that it may not work.** Most people who trade lose money in the long run. If the numbers do
   not add up after a hundred trades, it is fine to stick with long-term investing.

<Callout type="risk" title="The most important thing, once more">
  Trading is risky and no plan, indicator or course guarantees a profit. Risk
  only money you can afford to lose, do not use leverage until you have long
  experience, and do not trust anyone who promises a sure income.
</Callout>
