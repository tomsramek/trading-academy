From the first course you know that <KeyTerm term="support">support</KeyTerm> is a level where a fall
stopped in the past, and <KeyTerm term="resistance">resistance</KeyTerm> a level where a rise stopped.
Now we look at why that happens and how to work with it without relying on one magic number.

## Why it works

Behind every level there is **human memory and waiting orders**. Someone who bought at the top and
then watched a loss is happy to sell once the price comes back "at least to break even". Someone who
missed the purchase at the low waits for a second chance. Round numbers (70,000, 100,000) also
attract lots of limit orders.

None of this is a law of nature. It is just many people looking at the same chart and acting alike.

## A zone, not a line

The price rarely returns to a level to the exact dollar. That is why you draw a **zone** – a band
between several similar highs or lows.

Look at Bitcoin in 2024. Between March and July the rise stopped five times in a band between roughly 70,000 and 73,800: at
73,777, 72,798, 71,979, 71,997 and 70,080. Anyone waiting for exactly 73,777 would not understand why
the price turned thousands of dollars lower.

## Breakout and retest

A breakout happens when the price **closes** beyond the zone – not when a candle's wick crosses it for
a moment. On 6 November 2024 the daily candle closed at 75,572, above the whole zone, and on high
volume (more on volume in module 2).

After a breakout the price often comes back to "test" the zone. This is called a retest. And here
comes the interesting part: a <KeyTerm term="role-reversal">role reversal</KeyTerm>. Former resistance
can become support. In April 2025 Bitcoin fell from more than 100,000 to 74,508 – and stopped just
above the zone where sellers had stopped it five times a year earlier.

## False breakouts

Not every breakout holds. In August 2024 the price fell within a single day to 49,000 – far below the support around 56,500 that had already cracked in July. Anyone who sold "because support broke" sold almost at the bottom: three
days later Bitcoin was back above 61,000.

How do you protect yourself from false breakouts? There is no certainty, but it helps to:

- wait for a **candle close** beyond the zone, not the first wick,
- watch whether the breakout comes with **higher volume**,
- use a stop-loss that allows for the market coming back.

<Callout type="warning" title="A level is not a wall">
  Support and resistance show where people made decisions in the past. Every
  level breaks sooner or later – and the more often the price tests it, the more
  of the waiting orders have already been used up.
</Callout>

## Common myths

- **"Support is at 56,500, full stop."** It is a zone, and even a zone can break.
- **"A wick above resistance = a breakout."** The candle close decides, not a brief excursion.
- **"The more touches, the stronger the level."** Often the opposite – each touch uses up some of the
  waiting orders.

<Callout title="Summary">

- Support and resistance work because people make decisions based on the same past prices.
- Draw zones from several similar highs or lows, not one exact line.
- Breakout = a candle close beyond the zone; a retest and a role reversal often follow.
- False breakouts are common – allow for them in your stop-loss.

</Callout>
