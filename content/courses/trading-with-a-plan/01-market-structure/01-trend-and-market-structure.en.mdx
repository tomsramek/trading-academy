In the first course you saw that a trend shows in its highs and lows. Now we turn that into a tool.
Instead of a feeling that "it is probably going up", you mark specific points on the chart and use
them to say which phase the market is in. This is called <KeyTerm term="market-structure">market structure</KeyTerm>.

## Clear highs and lows

Price never moves in a straight line. It goes up, pulls back, goes up again. Every peak where the
price turned down is a **high**, every bottom where it bounced up is a **low**. They are also called
_swing highs_ and _swing lows_.

Do not count every small wiggle. Look for points you can see at first glance – for example the
highest price for several weeks on each side.

## Rising and falling structure

- **Rising structure** – every new high is above the previous one, and so is every low. Buyers are in
  control.
- **Falling structure** – lower highs and lower lows. Every attempt to rise ends lower than the last
  one.
- **Range** – highs and lows stay at roughly the same levels.

## Example: Bitcoin 2022–2023

2022 was a textbook decline. A high of 48,190 USDT at the end of March, then a lower high in May
(32,399) and another in August (25,211). The lows fell too: 26,700, 17,622 and, after the collapse of
the FTX exchange in November, as low as 15,476.

## When a trend breaks

A downtrend ends when the price breaks **the last lower high**. This is called a <KeyTerm term="break-of-structure">break of structure</KeyTerm>. On the chart above, the last lower
high was 18,388 from 14 December. On 12 January 2023 the daily candle closed above it (18,847) – and
eight days later also above the November high of 21,481.

A break of structure does not mean a big rally starts now. It is only **an early warning** that
sellers are losing strength. Look at March 2023: the price fell to 19,549 – below the February low.
The rising structure broke for a while, and yet the market reached 31,000 in about five weeks.

<Callout type="warning" title="Structure is a map, not a forecast">
  Structure tells you what has happened so far and helps you decide where you
  are wrong. It does not tell you what comes next. Any break can be false – that
  is why it always comes with a stop-loss.
</Callout>

## How to use it

- **Trade with the structure, not against it.** Buying "the bottom" in a falling structure is a bet
  against the trend.
- **Structure gives you a place for the stop-loss.** In a rising structure it typically goes below the
  last higher low – if the price breaks it, your idea no longer holds.
- **Start with a longer timeframe.** On an hourly chart you will find dozens of "breaks", on a daily
  chart only a few that matter.

## Common myths

- **"It is down a lot, it has to go up."** A low price is not a signal. In 2022 Bitcoin looked cheap
  at 30,000 – and then at 20,000.
- **"One higher low = a new trend."** One swallow does not make a summer. Wait for a break of
  structure.
- **"Structure always works."** March 2023 shows that even the nicest structure can break for a while.

<Callout title="Summary">

- Market structure = the clear highs and lows and their order.
- Rising: higher highs and lows. Falling: lower highs and lows.
- A break of structure is a break of the last lower (or higher) point – a warning, not a certainty.
- Above all, structure tells you where you are wrong, and that is where the stop-loss goes.

</Callout>
