This lesson does not aim to teach you to trade with leverage. It aims to show you **how it works and
why most people lose money on it** – so you know what you are getting into when an exchange offers you
a "100× bonus".

## Spot: you own the coin

On spot you buy the actual cryptocurrency. When the price falls by 30%, you have 30% less – but you still have the coins and can wait – though nothing guarantees the price comes back. The worst possible case is a fall to zero: you lose what you put
in, nothing more.

## Futures: a bet on the price

So-called <KeyTerm term="futures">futures</KeyTerm> are contracts on the price of a cryptocurrency. You do not
own the coin. In crypto the most traded are so-called perpetual futures – contracts with no expiry
date. They let you:

- **trade with leverage** – open a 10,000 € position with a 1,000 € deposit,
- **profit from a fall** (short) – which also means losing when the price rises.

The deposit that backs the position is called <KeyTerm term="margin">margin</KeyTerm>. And this is
where the trouble starts.

## Liquidation

When the price moves against you so far that the loss eats almost all the margin, the exchange closes
the position itself. This is called <KeyTerm term="liquidation">liquidation</KeyTerm>, and the margin
is gone.

With 10× leverage, a move of roughly 9–10% against you is enough. With 20× about 5%, with 50× just
under 2% – a normal Bitcoin move within a few hours.

An example from 5 March 2024: someone buys Bitcoin in the afternoon at 68,000 with 10× leverage. The
liquidation price is roughly 61,500. A few hours later the price is at 59,005 – the position is
liquidated and the margin is gone. By the end of the week Bitcoin is back at 69,000. Whoever bought on
spot is in profit. Whoever bought with leverage has nothing.

## Funding: a fee for holding

A perpetual contract has no expiry date. To keep its price close to the spot price, traders pay each
other <KeyTerm term="funding-rate">funding</KeyTerm>, usually every 8 hours. When the contract trades above the spot price (demand for longs, bets on a rise, is stronger), longs pay shorts; when it trades below, shorts pay longs. In euphoria that can be a lot.

A common baseline rate is 0.01% per 8 hours. That is 0.03% a day, roughly **11% a year of the position
value**. With 10× leverage that is over 100% of your margin a year – just for holding the position. In
the heat of a bull market funding is often many times higher.

## Regulation

In the EU, crypto derivatives are financial instruments, and regulated exchanges and brokers have to
follow rules that protect retail investors. For crypto CFDs leverage is limited to 2 : 1, and according
to analyses ESMA cited in 2018, 74–89% of retail investor accounts lose money on CFDs. Platforms
offering 50× or 100× leverage are usually based outside the EU and give no such protection.

<Callout type="risk" title="Leverage is not for beginners">
  With leverage you can lose your whole deposit within minutes – even if you are
  right about the long-term direction. Until you have long experience with spot,
  small amounts and a plan, leave futures alone.
</Callout>

## Common myths

- **"With 10× leverage I will make ten times more."** And lose ten times faster. Liquidation comes
  before profit.
- **"I will use a stop-loss, so I cannot be liquidated."** In a fast market the stop-loss fills at a
  worse price – and funding and fees keep running.
- **"A short is safe when I know it will fall."** The market can rise for longer than you can hold out.

<Callout title="Summary">

- On spot you own the coin and can lose at most your deposit; futures are a bet on the price.
- With 10× leverage liquidation comes after a 9–10% move, with higher leverage even sooner.
- Funding is paid every few hours and over a year can eat a large part of the margin.
- 50× leverage and more usually means a platform without EU protection – and, for a beginner, a fast
  road to zero.

</Callout>
